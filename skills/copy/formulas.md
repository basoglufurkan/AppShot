# Headline formulas

A formula is a skeleton for a headline, not a fill-in template. Choose the formula from the shot's job (see `sequence.md`), draft 3 variants, then run the winner through `rewrite-drills.md`. Every example below follows the Appshot caption rules: 2–6 words, `**word**` marks the accent (1–2 per headline), and numbers we can't verify are `{placeholders}`.

## Quick pick

| Shot job | Reach for first | Then try |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (shot 1) | 1 Outcome + timeframe, 3 Pain → relief, 4 X without Y | 23 Named feature + twist, 5 Identity, 17 Moment |
| Core value (shot 2) | 2 Verb + object + benefit, 12 Effort minimiser | 21 Proof of craft |
| Features (3–5) | 2, 11 Consolidation, 7 Number-led, 6 Contrast | 14 Enemy, 19 Curiosity |
| Differentiator | 4 X without Y, 23 Named feature, 5 Identity | 18 Familiar analogy, 24 Local anchor |
| Trust | 13 Social proof, 20 Zero list, 21 Proof of craft | 10 Control |
| Close | 22 Benefit pair, 16 Future self, 25 Habit | 11 Consolidation |

**Accent placement:** highlight the word that carries the payoff: the outcome, the number, or the differentiator. Never a pronoun, article, or the brand.

**Travel:** formulas 1, 2, 4, 7, 11, 12 and 22 survive translation almost everywhere. Formulas 8 (question), 9 (single-verb command), 15 (permission) and anything pun-based need per-locale judgement; check the locale file.

---

### 1. Outcome + timeframe
`[Result] [tonight / this week / by Sunday / in {n} days]`
- **Use when:** the product delivers a felt result fast, as in sleep, habits, budgeting set-up or first lessons.
- **Examples:** "Fall asleep **faster** tonight" · "A clear budget by **Sunday**" · "Your first song in **{days}** days"
- **Trap:** invented timeframes ("Fluent in 30 days"). If the duration isn't a product fact, use a relative time ("tonight", "this week") or drop it.

### 2. Verb + object + benefit
`[Verb] [object], [human payoff]`
- **Use when:** the feature is an action and you need a clause explaining why it matters.
- **Examples:** "Scan receipts, skip the **spreadsheet**" · "Snap a meal, see the **macros**" · "Record once, **share** everywhere"
- **Trap:** both halves are features ("Scan receipts, export CSV"). The second half must be something a person feels.

### 3. Pain → relief
`No more [pain]` · `[Pain]? [Relief].` · `[Pain], [solved/sorted]`
- **Use when:** the category is frustration-driven: bills, insomnia, clutter, forgotten passwords.
- **Examples:** "No more **late fees**" · "Inbox chaos, **sorted**" · "Lost receipts? **Found.**"
- **Trap:** dwelling on the pain, or blaming the reader ("Still broke?"). The relief must be as visible as the pain.

### 4. X without Y
`[Desired outcome] without [the usual sacrifice]`
- **Use when:** the category's known cost (gym, spreadsheet, ads, account, equipment) is exactly what you remove.
- **Examples:** "**Strong** without a gym" · "Budgeting without **spreadsheets**" · "Meditation without the **jargon**"
- **Trap:** a vague Y ("without stress"), or a Y that isn't true (hidden equipment, a mandatory account).

### 5. Identity
`For [specific people] who [specific trait]` · `Built for [role]`
- **Use when:** the app serves a niche, or the differentiator is "made for people like me".
- **Examples:** "For runners who hate **treadmills**" · "Built for **night-shift** nurses" · "For cooks with **10 minutes**"
- **Trap:** too broad ("For people who want to be healthy"), or excluding most of the audience ("For moms" when dads use it too). Gendered languages also make "for X who…" awkward. Check the locale file.

### 6. Contrast (from → to)
`From [before] to [after]` · `[Before] → [after]`
- **Use when:** there is a visible transformation (organising, editing, learning). Pairs with the `before-after` layout.
- **Examples:** "From chaos to **calm**" · "Messy notes → clear **plans**" · "Blurry to **sharp**, one tap"
- **Trap:** abstract pairs ("good to great"). Both ends should be on the screen.

### 7. Number-led
`[Real number] [unit], [benefit]`
- **Use when:** a count, speed or size is genuinely impressive *and verifiable*. Count it from the repo's data files or get it from the owner.
- **Examples:** "**{recipes}** recipes, all under 20 min" · "**{workouts}** workouts, zero equipment" · "**3 taps** to log a meal"
- **Trap:** invented numbers, and unimpressive ones ("12 templates"). Small numbers belong in a subhead or nowhere. Record where each number came from.

### 8. Question hook
`[A question the user already asks]?`
- **Use when:** the question *is* the search intent, and the screen visibly answers it.
- **Examples:** "Where did your **money** go?" · "What's for **dinner**?" · "Ready for **race day**?"
- **Trap:** yes/no questions with obvious answers ("Want to save money?"). In JA/KO/ZH, statements usually beat questions; in ES, add the opening ¿.

### 9. Single-verb command
`[Strong verb] your [domain]`
- **Use when:** a confident brand needs a short hook and the verb itself is the benefit.
- **Examples:** "**Own** your mornings" · "**Master** your macros" · "**Outsmart** your bills"
- **Trap:** soft verbs (manage, handle, enhance) and violent ones (crush, kill, dominate) that translate absurdly.

### 10. Control / ownership
`Your [X], your [rules / pace / way]`
- **Use when:** privacy, customisation or self-paced learning is the point, and the screen shows real control (toggles, custom plans).
- **Examples:** "Your money, your **rules**" · "Your pace, your **progress**" · "Your data stays **yours**"
- **Trap:** heavily overused. Use it once per set at most, and only over a screen that proves it.

### 11. Consolidation
`All your [named things], one [place / list / screen]`
- **Use when:** you replace several apps, tabs or tools.
- **Examples:** "Every subscription, one **list**" · "All your cards, one **wallet**" · "Notes, tasks and docs, **together**"
- **Trap:** "All-in-one" without naming the things. Name the specific items or the category.

### 12. Effort minimiser
`[Result] in [one tap / one photo / a sentence]`
- **Use when:** speed or ease is the differentiator.
- **Examples:** "Invoices in **one tap**" · "Plan a trip in a **sentence**" · "Background gone in **one tap**"
- **Trap:** "in seconds" is on every listing. Use a concrete unit (tap, photo, sentence) that's literally true.

### 13. Social proof lead
`[Verified proof] [audience]`
- **Use when:** it's the trust shot, or the proof is exceptional enough to be the hook. Wording rules are in `proof.md`.
- **Examples:** "Loved by **{users}** runners" · "**{rating}★** from {ratings_count} reviews" · "**{award}** {award_year}"
- **Trap:** unverified or stale numbers. Always write a no-number fallback caption alongside. **App Store only:** Google Play images can't carry ratings, counts, awards or quotes, so the Play set uses formula 20 or 21 here.

### 14. Enemy / stop
`Stop [guesswork / tedious thing]` · `Quit the [tool]`
- **Use when:** users already resent a method (spreadsheets, guessing, paper).
- **Examples:** "Stop guessing your **macros**" · "Quit the **spreadsheet**" · "Stop losing **receipts**"
- **Trap:** making the user the enemy ("Stop being lazy"). The enemy is a method or object, never the person.

### 15. Permission / reassurance
`[Small thing] counts` · `[Beginners] welcome` · `No [pressure]`
- **Use when:** fear or shame blocks the install: mental health, beginner fitness, language anxiety.
- **Examples:** "Rest days **count**" · "Beginners **welcome**" · "No streak **guilt**"
- **Trap:** too soft for performance categories. Rarely right for shot 1 unless the brand is wellness-first.

### 16. Future self
`Become [specific identity]` · `Be the [person] who [does X]`
- **Use when:** the app builds an identity or habit people aspire to.
- **Examples:** "Become a **morning** person" · "Be the friend who **remembers**" · "Your **calmer** self starts here"
- **Trap:** "the new you" clichés, and transformation promises that sound medical.

### 17. Moment / scenario
`When [moment], [action]` · `For [moment]`
- **Use when:** the app is a situational utility, and a moment makes the value concrete.
- **Examples:** "When the bill comes, **split it**" · "For 3 a.m. **thoughts**" · "Dinner ideas at **6 p.m.**"
- **Trap:** culture-bound moments (Thanksgiving, April tax day). Swap the moment per locale.

### 18. Familiar analogy
`A [known helper] in your pocket` · `Your [expert] for [situation]`
- **Use when:** the category is new or hard to explain (AI tutors, coaches, assistants).
- **Examples:** "A personal trainer in your **pocket**" · "Your **sous-chef** for weeknights" · "A **tutor** for every homework night"
- **Trap:** naming a competitor or brand ("Like Notion, but…") is a trademark and store-policy risk. Use "in your pocket" once per set at most.

### 19. Curiosity / reveal
`See what your [X] is [hiding]` · `[X], decoded`
- **Use when:** the app turns data into insight (sleep, spending, screen time).
- **Examples:** "See what your sleep is **hiding**" · "Find where the **hours** go" · "Your spending, **decoded**"
- **Trap:** clickbait that hides the category. At thumbnail size, the viewer must still know what the app is.

### 20. Zero list
`No [X]. No [Y]. Just [Z].` · `Zero [cost]`
- **Use when:** it's a trust or differentiator shot about ads, accounts, tracking or subscriptions.
- **Examples:** "No ads. No account. Just **notes**." · "Zero **tracking**" · "No sign-up. **Start** writing."
- **Trap:** every "No" must be verified in code and business model. Claiming "no tracking" while shipping an analytics SDK is a policy and trust violation.

### 21. Proof of craft
`Every [granular unit], [handled]` · `Down to the [unit]`
- **Use when:** a premium or pro tool needs detail to signal quality.
- **Examples:** "Every **calorie**, from one photo" · "Down to the **minute**" · "Every rep, every **set**"
- **Trap:** detail nobody searched for ("14 export formats"), unless that is the intent.

### 22. Benefit pair
`[Benefit A], [benefit B]` (parallel)
- **Use when:** two benefits naturally come together; good for closes and broad audiences.
- **Examples:** "Eat **better**, spend less" · "Sleep more, **stress** less" · "Learn faster, **remember** longer"
- **Trap:** two weak benefits don't add up to one strong one. Parallel forms can balloon in DE/FR, so recheck length after translation.

### 23. Named feature hero
`[Feature name] [does the job for you]`
- **Use when:** it's the differentiator shot, the feature is unique, and its name appears in the UI.
- **Examples:** "**Autopilot** builds your budget" · "**Smart Lists** sort themselves" · "**Focus Mode** silences the noise"
- **Trap:** a brand-new name means nothing on its own, so always attach the plain benefit. The name must match the localised UI label.

### 24. Local anchor
`[Benefit] for [real local context]`
- **Use when:** the app is genuinely localised: local banks, exams, transit, currency, prayer times.
- **Examples:** "Built for **UK** banks" · "Every **SAT** topic, covered" · "Prayer times for **your city**"
- **Trap:** claiming a local fit the product doesn't have, or using national symbols (see locale files).

### 25. Habit / minimum dose
`[Tiny unit] a day. [Result].`
- **Use when:** the app wins on low commitment (learning, journaling, fitness snacks).
- **Examples:** "One lesson a day. **That's it.**" · "A page a day, **real** progress" · "One photo a day, **forever**"
- **Trap:** the tiny unit must match the real session length in the app.
