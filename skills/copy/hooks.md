# Hooks: shot 1 and the search-result trio

Shot 1 is the most valuable piece of copy in the listing. Shots 2–3 decide whether the viewer believes it. Write these three first and best; everything after shot 3 is for people who are already interested.

## Where these shots are seen

- **App Store search results (iPhone):** the first three portrait screenshots appear side by side under the app name. If there is an app preview video, it shows with the first two screenshots; a landscape screenshot takes the whole row. Apple has long reported that most App Store downloads follow a search, so this row is the real first impression for most installs.
- **App Store product page:** about two and a half portrait screenshots are visible before any swipe.
- **Google Play:** the listing shows the screenshot carousel near the top (the feature graphic leads when a promo video exists). Some search layouts show screenshots too. Treat shots 1–3 as the billboard on both stores.
- **Apple Search Ads:** default ads are built from the first screenshots, so the hook is often literally the ad.

## The job of shot 1

In about two seconds, at thumbnail size, the viewer must answer three questions without effort:

1. **Is this what I searched for?** Category confirmation, with a word the searcher would use.
2. **What do I get?** The outcome, not the feature.
3. **Does it look good and trustworthy?** This is the design's job, but a sloppy caption can ruin it.

Rules:
- **One idea.** 2–5 words, the largest type in the set. No subhead carrying meaning, because subheads are unreadable in search thumbnails (see `length.md`).
- **The category or outcome word appears** in the headline (budget/money/spending for a budget app), in its natural form.
- **The screen is the "aha" screen:** the most valuable real state of the app, with realistic data. Never onboarding, login, splash, settings or an empty state (Apple guideline 2.3.3 also requires showing the app in use).
- **Don't spend shot 1 on the app name or logo.** The name is printed beside the icon. Exception: a brand famous enough that the name *is* the promise.
- **No puns or idioms in shot 1.** It has to survive 20+ locales; transcreate per locale.
- **Proof leads only when exceptional and verified** (see `proof.md`), and only on the App Store. Google Play images can't carry ratings, counts or awards, so the Play hook is always a benefit.

## Hook types, ranked

This ranking is for the common case: a known category and an unknown brand.

| Rank | Type | Example | Best when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Outcome promise** | "Wake up **rested**" | The category is understood; the outcome is the reason to install |
| 2 | **Pain relief** | "No more **late fees**" | Searches are driven by frustration |
| 3 | **Category + twist** | "A budget that **builds itself**" | The category is crowded; the twist is your differentiator |
| 4 | **Visual demo** | "**Erase** anything" | Photo, video and AR tools where the UI is the wow |
| 5 | **Identity** | "For **night-shift** nurses" | Vertical or niche apps |
| 6 | **Proof** | "**{rating}★** from {ratings_count} reviews" | Exceptional, current, verified numbers or awards (App Store only) |
| 7 | **Question** | "What's for **dinner**?" | The question is literally the query, and the screen answers it |

Adjust the ranking when:
- **Nobody searches the category yet** (a new kind of app): explain the category first ("Your AI **tutor** for maths"), outcome second.
- **The brand is dominant:** proof and brand can lead.
- **The app is a visual tool:** the demo can beat the outcome, with a 2–3 word label on the magic moment.

## Shots 2 and 3

- **Together with shot 1, they are one billboard.** They answer "why this one?" by covering the top three install reasons. A viewer who never swipes should still know the core value and the main differentiator.
- **Shot 2 = core value:** the main job done on the most-used screen. **Shot 3 = the strongest differentiator**, or the second biggest reason to install.
- **Each panel must stand alone** (it may be the only one fully visible) and still read as a sequence: left to right, or right to left in RTL locales.
- **Don't restate shot 1's promise** in new words. Each shot answers a different question (see `sequence.md`).
- **Keep caption size, position and style identical across 1–3** so the trio reads as one unit. A panoramic bridge from 1 to 2 invites the swipe.
- **Lineup test:** place your trio next to the top three competitors for the primary keyword. If your captions could be swapped onto their screenshots, rewrite.

## How to test hooks

### Before launch (cheap, do it every time)

1. **Thumbnail test.** Render shots 1–3 at the real search-result size (about a third of the phone width each) and look at them on a phone at arm's length. If the headline is not instantly readable or the category is unclear, it fails.
2. **Five-second test.** Show the trio for five seconds to someone who doesn't know the app, then ask what it does and who it's for. It passes if they name the category and the outcome in their own words. If they paraphrase better than you wrote it, use their phrasing.
3. **Competitor lineup.** Mock a search-results page with your trio among the top five apps for the primary keyword, and check whether yours is the one you would tap.
4. **Native read-aloud.** For each locale, a native speaker reads the hook aloud. Hesitation, or rewording while explaining it, means rewrite.

### After launch (live traffic)

- **Apple Product Page Optimization:**
  - Test up to three treatments against the original (screenshots, icon or previews), localised, for up to 90 days.
  - App Store Connect reports the conversion lift and the confidence level.
  - Change one variable per test: the hook caption, *or* the hook screen.
- **Apple Custom Product Pages:**
  - Build hook variants per keyword theme and point Apple Search Ads ad groups at them, then compare conversion by keyword.
  - Apple added keyword assignment for custom product pages in 2025, which lets them surface in organic search. Write theme-specific hooks for them, such as a "shared budget" page for couples' queries.
- **Google Play store listing experiments:**
  - Test up to three variants against the current listing.
  - Localised experiments can target specific languages; default-graphics experiments run across all of them.
  - Pick the metric (prefer retained first-time installers over raw installs), set a minimum detectable effect, and run at least seven days to cover a weekly cycle.
  - **Custom store listings** let a hook vary by country, campaign or pre-registration.
- **Reading results:**
  - Don't call a winner early, and don't test across holidays or feature placements.
  - Test big swings first (a different hook *type*), then wording, then the highlight word.
  - A winning hook in one locale is a hypothesis, not a fact, in another.
- **Log every test** in the plan: locale, variant text, hook type, dates, and the result against baseline. Start each test from a written hypothesis:
  > Because {insight}, changing the hook from {A, type} to {B, type} will raise first-time-installer conversion in {locale}.
