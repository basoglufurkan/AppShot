# Indonesian (Bahasa Indonesia)

One of the world's largest mobile markets: young, Android-dominant, price-sensitive and super-app-literate (Gojek, Grab, Shopee, Tokopedia). Clear, friendly, value-forward Indonesian wins. Malay vocabulary and stiff formal Indonesian lose.

## Voice and register

- **"kamu"** (informal you) is standard for young consumer apps: e-commerce, social, games, fitness, education.
- **"Anda"** (formal, always capitalised) fits banking, insurance, government, healthcare and B2B. Never write lowercase "anda".
- **Many apps sidestep the pronoun** with imperatives or noun phrases ("Atur keuangan", "Semua pengeluaran tercatat").
- **Casual particles** (*yuk*, *nih*, *dong*, *kok*) and *bahasa gaul* (nggak, banget) are common in youth brands' copy. They are great for playful apps and wrong for finance or health. Never mix casual particles with "Anda".
- **Tone:** upbeat, helpful, value-conscious. Indonesians respond well to *hemat* (thrifty), *praktis* (practical) and *aman* (safe).

## Typography rules

- **Latin script, no diacritics.** Sentence case is standard in modern app copy. PUEBI-style Title Case (capitalise every word except particles: di, ke, dari, dan, yang, untuk) also appears in ads. Choose one, consistently; never ALL CAPS.
- **"di" spacing is the classic error:**
  - The preposition is separate: "di rumah", "di mana saja".
  - The passive prefix is attached: "dibuat", "disimpan".
  - The same applies to "ke" (preposition) vs "ke-" (ordinal prefix).
- **Numbers:** decimal comma, thousands point. Currency "Rp49.000" (no space, per PUEBI); "Rp49 rb" is informal shorthand. Dates "4 November 2026"; times "21.30".
- **Reduplication** for plurals ("aplikasi-aplikasi") is long and unnecessary; context implies plural.
- **Quotes:** “ ”.

## Length

- Expect **+10–25% characters** versus English. Affixed words grow quickly ("pengeluaran", "mempersonalisasikan").
- Headline target ≤ 30 characters.
- Shorten with:
  - "tanpa + noun" ("tanpa iklan", "tanpa ribet")
  - dropping "dengan mudah dan cepat"
  - "jadi" constructions ("Keuangan jadi rapi")
  - accepted loanwords (app/aplikasi, gym, diet)

## Store and search habits

- **Play is the primary listing** (Android has the vast majority of devices). Many users have low-to-mid-range phones and limited data: "hemat kuota" (saves data), "ringan" (lightweight) and "bisa offline" are real benefits.
- **Queries:**
  - money: "aplikasi keuangan", "catatan keuangan", "pengatur keuangan"
  - health and fitness: "diet", "olahraga di rumah", "hitung kalori"
  - learning, food and creative: "belajar bahasa Inggris", "resep masakan", "edit foto"
  - religious: "Al-Quran", "jadwal sholat"
- **Payments:** GoPay, OVO, DANA, ShopeePay, QRIS and carrier billing. Cards are less common, so free value should be visible before any trial wall.
- **Value words that convert:** "gratis", "hemat", "promo", "cashback" (retail contexts), "tanpa iklan". Use them only when true.
- **Seasonality:** Ramadan and Lebaran (Idul Fitri) are the biggest commercial season; *harbolnas* (12.12) for shopping. Use custom product pages.

## Pitfalls

- **Malaysian Malay vocabulary** (the languages are close, but words differ):

  | Word | Malaysian meaning | Indonesian meaning |
  |---|---|---|
  | *percuma* | free | in vain |
  | *kereta* | car | train |
  | *boleh* | can | allowed |

  Other Malaysian giveaways: *telefon bimbit* ("ponsel"/"HP" in Indonesia), *wang* ("uang" in Indonesia).
- **Mixed "kamu" and "Anda"**, or lowercase "anda".
- **"di" spacing errors** ("dirumah", "di buat").
- **English-Indonesian code-mixing overload** ("Track pengeluaran kamu dengan smart"). One loanword per headline.
- **Finance is sensitive.** Illegal online lending (*pinjol ilegal*) has made users wary. Finance apps must be precise about OJK licensing before claiming "aman" or "terdaftar".
- **Gambling (*judi*) is strictly illegal.** Avoid casino-like visuals. Food imagery should be halal-safe for broad audiences.
