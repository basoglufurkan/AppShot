# Swedish (Svenska)

Swedes are fluent in English and digitally mature: Spotify, Klarna and BankID are home turf. A Swedish listing wins by sounding effortlessly local and *lagom*. Hype backfires.

## Voice and register

- **"du" for everyone.** The du-reformen made informal address universal decades ago. Formal "ni" to one person sounds old-fashioned or ironic; use it only as the plural.
- **Lagom and Jante:** understated, honest, egalitarian. "Smidigt" (smooth, handy), "enkelt" and "koll" (overview) are beloved marketing words. Superlatives and "världens bästa" read as American bragging.
- **Imperatives** ("Spara", "Planera", "Träna") and noun phrases ("Koll på ekonomin") are both natural. Light, warm humour works.
- **Sustainability and fairness** are real values; claims must be accurate.

## Typography rules

- **Quotes:** ”…” uses the *same* right-double-quote glyph on both sides, which is distinctive Swedish style. ’…’ for nested quotes. »…» appears in books.
- **Dash:** spaced en dash (" – ").
- **Sentence case.** Å Ä Ö must render. Check that the display font doesn't fall back on å.
- **Numbers:** "49 kr" (kronor after, with a space), comma decimal ("4,5"), space as the thousands separator ("10 000"). Dates "4 november" (lowercase months); times "21.30" or "21:30".
- **Compounds are written together.** *Särskrivning* (splitting compounds) is the classic error that changes meaning and invites ridicule: "kycklinglever" (chicken liver) vs "kyckling lever" (chicken lives). In tight layouts, use a soft hyphen at the compound seam; never insert a space.

## Length

- Expect **+5–20% characters** versus English. Compounds are long ("budgetplanering", "träningsprogram") but replace several English words.
- Headline target ≤ 30 characters.
- Shorten with "koll på", "utan + noun" ("utan reklam", "utan krångel") and a single strong verb.

## Store and search habits

- **Queries:**
  - money: "budget", "ekonomi", "spara pengar"
  - health and fitness: "kaloriräknare", "träning", "löpning", "stegräknare", "meditation", "sömn"
  - food and everyday: "recept", "inköpslista", "väder"
- **Many Swedes search in English too**, so echo one English category term only if the UI uses it.
- **National infrastructure as trust signals** (only if integrated):
  - "Logga in med BankID" for finance, health and government-adjacent apps
  - **Swish**, the P2P payment, used as a verb ("swisha")
  - Klarna
- **Subscriptions are normal.** Swedes pay for apps, but expect honesty: "gratis", "utan bindningstid" (no lock-in) and "avsluta när du vill" convert when true.
- **Local reference style:** Swedish market leaders (Spotify, Klarna, Kry, Tibber) use clean layouts, short friendly sentences and plenty of white space.

## Pitfalls

- **Särskrivning**, anglicisms ("tracka", "booka") outside youth brands, and hype.
- **False friends:**
  - *gift* (married, also poison), *rolig* (fun), *eventuellt* (possibly)
  - *aktuell* (current), *fack* (union/compartment), *semester* (holiday)
- **Danish or Norwegian forms** from MT or careless editing.
- **"ni" to a single user**, and "Du" capitalised mid-sentence.
- **Marknadsföringslagen** (Konsumentverket) prohibits misleading "gratis", unsubstantiated "bäst" and greenwashing. Environmental claims need proof.
