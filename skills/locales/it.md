# Italian (Italiano)

Italian readers respond to warmth, style and a sense of craft. Copy that sounds like a translated manual kills the aesthetic promise.

## Voice and register

- **"tu" is the norm** in Italian app and consumer marketing, including most fintech and many banks.
- **"Lei"** (formal, capitalised in formal writing) fits traditional banking, insurance, healthcare and public services. Follow the in-app strings.
- **Imperatives are natural and lively:** "Scopri", "Organizza", "Risparmia", "Allenati", "Impara". Nominal headlines are elegant: "Ogni spesa sotto controllo".
- **Tone:** warm, a little passionate, aesthetically aware. Words like *bello*, *finalmente*, *senza pensieri* carry emotion. Avoid bureaucratic Italian ("effettuare", "procedere", "usufruire").
- **Gender:** address the reader without gendered adjectives where possible ("Si parte!", "Tutto pronto?" instead of "Pronto/a?").

## Typography rules

- **Sentence case.** No Title Case.
- **Accents and elision:**
  - È (capital) is written with the accent, never "E'". Use "perché" with an acute accent, "è" with a grave.
  - Elide articles: "l'app", "un'app" (app is feminine, so "la tua app"), "dell'anno".
- **Quotes:** « » in traditional print, “ ” common in digital. Choose one.
- **Numbers:** "4,99 €" or "€ 4,99" (both common; match the app), comma decimal, thousands with a point ("1.000"). Times "21:30"; dates "4 novembre" (months lowercase).
- **Line breaks:** don't end a line with *di, a, da, in, con, e, il, la*; keep elided forms together ("l'app" never splits).

## Length

- Expect **+15–25% characters** versus English.
- Headline target ≤ 30 characters.
- Shorten with:
  - noun phrases ("Le tue finanze, in ordine")
  - "senza + noun" ("senza pubblicità", "senza stress")
  - dropping "in modo semplice e veloce"
- Italians accept English tech words (app, chat, smartphone, feed, coach), which are often shorter than the Italian equivalent.

## Store and search habits

- **Queries:**
  - money: "gestione spese", "budget", "risparmio"
  - fitness and health: "conta calorie", "contapassi", "dieta", "allenamento a casa", "palestra", "meditazione"
  - learning, food and home: "imparare l'inglese", "ricette", "lista della spesa", "meteo"
  - "gratis" is a common modifier.
- **Payments:** Satispay and PayPal are familiar. Subscription fatigue is real, so "Disdici quando vuoi" and "senza vincoli" reassure.
- **Food, design and beauty apps face expert critics.** Mock data must be authentic: real Italian dishes and correct names, no "spaghetti bolognese" or pineapple pizza.
- **Local reference style:** Italian market leaders mix warm photography and clean type, with emotional benefit headlines over feature lists.

## Pitfalls

- **False friends:**
  - *eventualmente* (possibly), *attualmente* (currently), *libreria* (bookshop/bookshelf), *parenti* (relatives)
  - *camera* (room), *argomento* (topic), *pretendere* (demand), *morbido* (soft)
- **"E'" instead of "È"**, apostrophe misuse ("un app" vs "un'app"), and missing accents.
- **Bureaucratic MT verbs:** effettuare, usufruire, procedere, provvedere.
- **AGCM** (antitrust and consumer authority) fines misleading claims, including "gratis" on subscription apps and unsubstantiated "il migliore". Health claims are regulated by the Ministry of Health.
- **Anglicism overload** ("Tracka i tuoi workout con insight smart") reads as cheap. One loanword per headline.
