# Portuguese (Brasil)

A huge, mobile-first, Android-heavy market that rewards warmth, informality and clear value. Write Brazilian Portuguese, never European Portuguese.

## Voice and register

- **"você" is the standard** neutral-friendly address for all national copy. Regional *tu* (South, parts of the Northeast) should not appear in national copy. *O senhor/a senhora* is too formal even for most banks.
- **Imperative uses the você (subjunctive) form:** "Descubra", "Comece", "Economize", "Organize", "Baixe". The colloquial *tu*-imperative ("Vem", "Bora", "Faz") is common in playful Brazilian ads. Use it only for brands that talk that way in the UI, and don't mix the two forms in one set.
- **Tone:** close, human, upbeat. Brazilian fintechs popularised a plain, friendly, anti-bureaucracy voice ("sem burocracia", "sem letras miúdas", "sem tarifas"). Humour and emotion are welcome; coldness reads as distrust.
- **"A gente"** (we, informal) works in subheads for friendly brands ("A gente cuida dos lembretes").

## Typography rules

- **Sentence case.** Accents (ã õ ç é ê á í ó ú â ô) must render; check display-font coverage for ã and õ.
- **Money:** "R$ 19,90" (space after R$, comma decimal); thousands with a point ("R$ 1.299,00"). Prices go stale and invite comparison, so avoid them in captions.
- **Time and dates:** "21h30" or "21:30"; dates "4 de novembro" (months lowercase).
- **Quotes:** “ ”. Exclamation marks are more accepted than in Europe, but use at most one per set and never on the hook.
- **Spelling:** follow the 2009 Acordo Ortográfico (e.g., "autoestima", "ideia" without an accent, "voo").
- **Line breaks:** keep "R$" with its number; don't end a line with *de, da, do, e, em*.

## Length

- Expect **+20–30% characters** versus English.
- Headline target ≤ 30 characters.
- Shorten with:
  - noun phrases ("Seu dinheiro, no controle")
  - "sem + noun" ("Sem anúncios", "Sem taxas")
  - dropping "de forma rápida e fácil"
- "Aplicativo" can usually become "app" or disappear.

## Store and search habits

- **Android dominates**, so Play (pt-BR) is often the primary listing. Many users have mid-range phones and limited data plans: "app leve", "funciona offline" and "economiza dados" are real benefits if true.
- **Queries:**
  - money: "controle financeiro", "planilha de gastos", "organizar finanças"
  - health and fitness: "contar calorias", "jejum intermitente", "treino em casa", "academia"
  - learning, home and tools: "aprender inglês", "receitas fáceis", "lista de compras", "editor de fotos"
  - Brazilian users often search the word *planilha* (spreadsheet) when they want a budgeting tool.
- **Money culture:**
  - **Pix** (instant payments) is universal; mention Pix if the app supports it.
  - **Boleto** remains a common way to pay bills.
  - *Parcelamento* (instalments) shapes e-commerce.
  - Price sensitivity is high: "grátis" and "sem tarifas" convert only when honest. Trial wording ("teste grátis") belongs on the paywall, not in screenshots.
- **Social proof** carries weight (reviews, "mais de {users} brasileiros"). WhatsApp is the default sharing channel: "Compartilhe no WhatsApp" is a natural feature line if real.
- **Local reference style:** Brazil's top apps (Nubank, iFood, Mercado Livre, PicPay) use bold colour, very short você-imperatives and friendly microcopy.

## Pitfalls

- **European Portuguese leaks:** telemóvel (celular), ecrã (tela), utilizador (usuário), registo (registro), equipa (equipe/time), "estás"/"tu" forms, and "a fazer" for the gerund ("fazendo").
- **False friends:**
  - *esquisito* (weird, not exquisite), *puxar* (pull), *pretender* (intend), *parentes* (relatives)
  - *assistir* (watch), *propaganda* (just "advertising" in BR, not pejorative)
- **Spanish interference** in MT (e.g., "aplicación", "fácil y rápido").
- **Overly formal MT:** "Realize o gerenciamento de suas finanças". Rewrite: "Organize suas finanças".
- **CONAR and the CDC (Código de Defesa do Consumidor)** police misleading claims and "grátis". Health claims fall under ANVISA, and privacy claims under LGPD.
- **Gendered reader adjectives** ("Bem-vindo", "Pronto?"). Rephrase: "Boas-vindas", "Tudo pronto?".
