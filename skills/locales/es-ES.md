# Spanish (España)

Peninsular Spanish for the Spain storefront only. Latin America is served by es-MX (App Store) and es-419 (Play). Don't reuse one file for both: vocabulary differences are immediately visible.

## Voice and register

- **"tú" is the norm** in Spanish app marketing, including most banks, telcos and retailers. *Usted* sounds distant or elderly-oriented; reserve it for apps whose UI already uses it (some insurance, medical or public-sector apps).
- **Address one person.** Avoid *vosotros* plurals in captions ("Descubrid"). The reader is alone with their phone.
- **Imperatives sound natural and energetic:** "Ahorra", "Organiza", "Descubre", "Controla". Nominal headlines are also idiomatic: "Tus cuentas, en orden".
- **Tone:** warm, a bit of humour and colloquial colour are welcome ("sin líos", "en un pispás" for playful brands). Spain responds to a human, slightly cheeky voice more than to corporate polish.
- **Inclusive first person plural** works as a soft call: "¿Empezamos?", "Vamos a ello".

## Typography rules

- **Opening ¿ and ¡ are mandatory:** "¿Listo para empezar?", "¡Por fin!". Missing them is an English/MT tell.
- **Sentence case only.** Spanish never Title Cases headlines.
- **Quotes:** « » is RAE-preferred, “ ” common in digital, ‘ ’ nested. Be consistent.
- **Numbers:** "4,99 €" with a space before €; decimal comma; thousands with a point ("1.000"; RAE also allows a space for 5+ digits); "50 %" with a space (RAE) or "50%" in display, consistently. Times 24 h ("21:30"); dates "4 de noviembre".
- **Accents:** keep accents on capitals ("ÉXITO", "ÁNIMO"). Watch tú (you) vs tu (your), más (more) vs mas, sí (yes) vs si (if).
- **Line breaks:** don't strand articles and prepositions (el, la, de, en, y) at a line end; keep "¿…?" phrases together.

## Length

- Expect **+20–30% characters** versus English, from articles and longer verb forms.
- Headline target ≤ 30 characters.
- Shorten with:
  - nominal phrases ("Tu dinero, bajo control")
  - dropping articles where natural
  - "sin + noun" ("sin anuncios", "sin complicaciones")
  - moving "de forma fácil y rápida" type padding out entirely

## Store and search habits

- **Queries:** "control de gastos", "gastos e ingresos", "contador de calorías", "ayuno intermitente", "entrenamiento en casa", "aprender inglés", "recetas fáciles", "lista de la compra", "escáner pdf", "meditación". "gratis" is a frequent modifier.
- **Payment and money culture:** Bizum is the everyday way to send money between friends. Bill-splitting and wallet apps should mention it if they integrate it (trademark, so use only if real). Price-conscious market with low tolerance for opaque subscriptions; "sin permanencia" (no lock-in) is a familiar telecom phrase that signals fairness.
- **Local life cues:** late dinners, *tapas*, *la compra semanal*, *el gimnasio* vs *entrenar en casa*. Use them in imagery and mock data (euro amounts, Spanish names, Spanish supermarket items).
- **Local reference style:** Spain's top apps use friendly *tú* imperatives, bright colours and short, colloquial headlines.

## Pitfalls

- **Latin American vocabulary in a Spain listing:** celular (móvil), computadora (ordenador), carro (coche), departamento (piso), manejar (conducir), platicar (hablar), jugo (zumo), lentes (gafas). Also *ustedes* where Spain says *vosotros*.
- **False friends:**
  - *actualmente* (currently), *embarazada* (pregnant), *librería* (bookshop), *éxito* (success)
  - *constipado* (has a cold), *molestar* (bother), *carpeta* (folder)
- **MT tells:** overuse of "realizar", "de forma sencilla", "usted" mixed with "tú", and missing ¿¡.
- **Gendered adjectives** aimed at the reader ("¿Listo?", "Bienvenido"). Rephrase: "¿Todo listo?", "Te damos la bienvenida". Avoid "@" and "-e" inclusive forms; RAE rejects them and many readers find them divisive.
- **Advertising law** (Ley General de Publicidad, consumer law) penalises misleading "gratis" and unsubstantiated superlatives. Health claims are tightly regulated.
