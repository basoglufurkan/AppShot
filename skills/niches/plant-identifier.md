# Plant Identifier & Care

## The searcher

- A new plant parent whose plant is drooping or yellowing — worried, guilty, searching "why are my leaves yellow".
- A walker or hiker who spots a flower or tree and wants its name right now.
- The gardener planning beds, fighting pests and wanting a schedule.
- A cat or dog owner worried whether a plant at home is toxic.
- After installing they want to feel: competent, curious satisfied, plants thriving instead of dying.

## What wins the install

1. **Instant ID from a photo** — the magic moment: camera on a leaf, name and photo match in seconds.
2. **Save a struggling plant** — diagnosis plus clear treatment steps is the strongest emotional job in the niche.
3. **Care that's personal** — watering and light adjusted to the plant, pot and room.
4. **Reminders that keep plants alive** — the reason people keep the app after the first scan.
5. **Safety** — toxicity for pets and kids, clearly flagged.
6. **Curiosity & collection** — "my garden" gallery and fun facts.

## Supporting elements

- **ID result card**: "{plantName} · {confidence}% match" — confidence as the app actually reports it.
- **Care chips**: "Water every {days} days", "Bright, indirect light", "{temp}° ideal".
- **Diagnosis card**: cause + numbered treatment steps.
- **Notifications**: "Time to water your {plantName}", "Fertilise {plantName} this week".
- **Toxicity badge**: "Toxic to cats and dogs" / "Pet-safe".
- **Light meter gauge** with a room label.
- **Garden grid** with small health indicators.

## Visual direction

- Lush and natural: deep forest greens, fresh mint and warm cream; natural-light photography of real plants (shot by the team or licensed).
- A botanical serif for headlines (like a field guide), a friendly sans for UI.
- Scan frames and result cards with soft shadows over real leaves; keep the photo authentic, not over-saturated.
- Leaders: PictureThis (camera-first, results-heavy), Planta (friendly, care-schedule-led), Blossom, PlantNet (scientific, community).
- Indoor-plant apps show cosy interiors; garden apps show outdoor beds and seasons.
- Keep text large; plant names in Latin can be small secondary lines.

## Avoid

- Accuracy absolutes ("99% accurate", "identifies any plant") — Apple 2.3.1; show confidence honestly.
- Edibility or foraging claims — never suggest eating or medicinal use of identified plants; physical-harm risk (Apple 1.4.1).
- Apple 4.3 (spam): identifier apps are heavily cloned; screenshots must show what's unique.
- Stock photos the app didn't actually identify, or results that don't match the photo.
- Toxicity shown as a guarantee — label it guidance and point to a vet in emergencies.
- Prices or "free" when scans or diagnosis are paywalled (2.3.7).
- Conversion mistakes: tiny result text, a paywall or onboarding as shot 1, generic leaf icons instead of real plants.
