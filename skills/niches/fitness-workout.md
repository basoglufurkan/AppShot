# Gym & Strength Training

## The searcher

- A gym-goer tired of tracking lifts in a notes app who wants a fast log and a real plan.
- A beginner intimidated by the gym floor who wants to be told exactly what to do today, with form guidance.
- A plateaued lifter searching "progressive overload" or "workout planner" to start gaining again.
- What they want to feel: "I walk in knowing the plan, and I can see myself getting stronger."

## What wins the install

1. **"Know exactly what to do today."** A clear plan-of-the-day screen kills gym anxiety, the main reason beginners quit.
2. **Visible progress.** PRs, volume charts and strength curves are the dopamine of this niche. Numbers sell better than bodies.
3. **Logging speed.** Show set rows filled in with one tap between sets. "Logging is a hassle" is the objection to beat.
4. **Adaptive programming.** An AI or algorithmic coach that adjusts weights and volume feels like having a personal trainer.
5. **Form guidance.** Exercise videos and muscle maps reassure beginners and signal quality.
6. **Ecosystem fit.** Watch logging and Health sync matter to serious lifters.

## Supporting elements

- PR notification: "New PR · {exercise} {weight} × {reps}".
- Volume chart card: "+{percent}% volume in {weeks} weeks", taken from the demo account's actual logged data.
- Set row card: "Set 3 · {weight} kg × {reps} · RPE {rpe}" with a checkmark.
- Rest timer ring counting down "1:30".
- Muscle heatmap on a neutral anatomical figure (chest and triceps lit).
- Coach bubble: "Increase bench to {weight} kg next session".
- Consistency calendar with workout days filled. An official "Works with Apple Health" or Health Connect badge, per guidelines only.

## Visual direction

- High energy: volt (neon lime on black), ember (orange-red), midnight. Brutal suits hardcore powerlifting brands.
- Typography: bold condensed uppercase sans with huge tabular numerals for weights and PRs.
- Tilt devices for dynamism. Use moody gym photography or athlete cutouts behind the device, licensed and diverse, with realistic strong bodies rather than only shredded physiques.
- Leaders use dark UIs with neon accents, muscle maps, big PR numbers and set tables pulled out large. Fitness-magazine clichés (oiled torsos) feel dated and alienate beginners.

## Avoid

- Before/after transformation photos and claims like "get abs in 30 days" or "gain 5 kg of muscle in a month". These are misleading health claims under Apple 1.4.1 / 2.3.1 and Google Play's health misinformation and deceptive behavior policies, and most ad networks reject them too.
- Sexualized fitness imagery, or showing only one body type.
- Steroid, SARM or supplement promotion, and medical claims ("fix your back pain").
- Apple Watch and Apple Health marks outside Apple's marketing guidelines. No Wear OS devices in iOS screenshots (Apple 2.3.10).
- Fake PRs, testimonials, trainer credentials or "{users}" counts that aren't real.
- Subscription prices, "7-day free trial" or discount banners in screenshots.
- Conversion killers: an exercise list as the hook, tiny log tables nobody can read, and generic gym stock photos that could advertise any app.
