# Homework Solver

## The searcher

- A student stuck on problem 14 at 10 pm with nobody to ask — frustrated, tired, close to giving up.
- A parent trying to help with math they last saw twenty years ago and not wanting to look lost in front of their child.
- A university student facing calculus, physics or chemistry problem sets and wanting to see *how*, not just *what*.
- After installing they want to feel: "I get it now — I could solve the next one myself."

## What wins the install

1. **Photo → solution in one move** — the magic is the camera; the first shot must show a real handwritten or printed problem turning into an answer.
2. **Steps you understand** — kills the parent/teacher objection "it's just cheating" and the student fear "I still won't get it on the test".
3. **Ask "why?"** — a follow-up chat on any step is what modern AI solvers win on.
4. **Coverage** — math first, then physics, chemistry, biology, essays; breadth reassures.
5. **Handles messy input** — handwriting, crooked photos, word problems.
6. **Check & practise** — verify your own answer, then try a similar problem.

## Story (shot by shot)

1. **Hook** — snap it, solved — show: camera frame on a handwritten problem next to the solved result — layout: before-after — angle: real paper texture vs crisp digital answer.
2. **Steps** — see exactly how — show: step-by-step solution — layout: zoom-callout — angle: zoom on the step most people get wrong, highlighted.
3. **Ask why** — your tutor never gets tired — show: follow-up chat on one step — layout: chat-bubbles — angle: student question + clear short explanation.
4. **Every subject** — one camera for all homework — show: subject picker or mixed results — layout: screen-grid — angle: math, physics, chemistry, biology tiles.
5. **Visualise** — graphs that make sense — show: graph/visualiser of a function — layout: hero-device — angle: the curve big and colourful.
6. **Practise** — check your answer, try another — show: answer check + similar problems — layout: floating-cards — angle: "Correct" chip and next-problem card.
7. **Handwriting** (optional) — show: messy handwriting recognised — layout: feature-list — angle: proof points (handwriting, word problems, textbooks).

## Supporting elements

- **Scan frame**: corner brackets over a handwritten equation on lined paper.
- **Step card**: "Step {n}: factor the quadratic" with the key move highlighted in the accent colour.
- **Chat bubbles**: student "Why do we divide both sides by 2?" → short, friendly explanation.
- **Subject chips**: Algebra · Geometry · Calculus · Physics · Chemistry · Biology.
- **Answer check chip**: "Correct" / "Almost — check step 3".
- **Similar problem card**: "Try one like this".
- **Stat**: "{subjects} subjects" — only from the real feature list.

## Visual direction

- Clean, bright and trustworthy: white or very light backgrounds, a confident blue or energetic volt accent, red only for errors.
- The contrast between analogue (real photographed paper, pencil handwriting) and digital (crisp rendered steps) *is* the story — keep both in shot 1.
- Render math with proper typesetting at large sizes; a fraction must be readable in the search-results thumbnail.
- Highlight the critical step with a marker-style accent, not a busy arrow cloud.
- Leaders (Photomath, Gauth, Brainly, Symbolab) keep UI minimal and formulas huge; follow that discipline.
- For a younger audience use friendlier rounded type; for university keep it sharp and technical.

## Avoid

- Cheating framing: "Never do homework again", "Copy the answers", "Get through the exam" — reviewers, parents and schools react badly, and it invites low ratings. Lead with understanding.
- Wrong solutions in screenshots — double-check every step; one visible mistake destroys trust.
- "100% accurate" or "solves any problem" — unsubstantiated claims (Apple 2.3.1).
- Copyrighted problems: textbook pages, publisher worksheets, real official exam questions (exam boards often assert copyright on their questions). Write your own problems.
- Exam-cheating context: no "use it during the test" or smartwatch-in-exam imagery.
- If the app has AI chat, keep its age rating and content filters consistent with what the screenshots promise to under-18s.
- Conversion mistakes: a typed equation instead of the camera, a subject list as shot 1, tiny formulas, more than one problem per shot.
