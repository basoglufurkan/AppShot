# Budget & Expense Tracker

## The searcher

- End of the month, staring at the balance: "Where did my money go?" — slightly anxious, wanting clarity rather than a lecture.
- A goal with a date: a house deposit, a wedding, a trip, an emergency fund.
- A couple merging finances or a family trying to stop arguing about spending.
- In high-inflation markets: tracking every purchase because prices move monthly.
- After installing they want to feel: in control, calm, and free to spend on what matters without guilt.

## What wins the install

1. **Clarity in one glance** — a single screen that shows where the money went (category breakdown) answers the searcher's exact question.
2. **Effortless tracking** — bank sync with auto-categorisation, or two-tap manual entry; kills "I'll never keep this up".
3. **"What's left" over "what's spent"** — remaining budget per category is the number people act on.
4. **Goals that move** — progress rings turn saving into a game you can win.
5. **Alerts that save money** — upcoming bills, overspending, forgotten subscriptions.
6. **Privacy & security** — encryption, read-only access, or "no bank login needed" for manual-first apps (turn it into a selling point).
7. **Budget together** — shared budgets for couples and families.

## Story (shot by shot)

1. **Hook** — see where your money goes — show: monthly overview with category donut — layout: hero-device — angle: one clear month, sample data, calm colours.
2. **Effortless** — log in two taps / syncs automatically — show: add-expense sheet or synced transactions — layout: ui-pieces — angle: keypad, category chip, merchant row lifted out of the UI.
3. **What's left** — know what you can still spend — show: category budgets with remaining amounts — layout: big-number — angle: "{remaining} left for dining".
4. **Goals** — watch your savings grow — show: savings goals with progress — layout: floating-cards — angle: 2–3 goal cards with rings.
5. **Alerts** — never miss a bill — show: bill/overspend/subscription notifications — layout: notification-stack — angle: three helpful, non-scary alerts.
6. **Insight** — spot trends early — show: monthly trend/report — layout: before-after — angle: last month vs this month, user's own numbers.
7. **Trust** — your data stays safe — show: accounts/security screen — layout: feature-list — angle: encryption, read-only, export, Face ID — only what's true.
8. **Proof** (optional) — show: verified rating — layout: social-proof — angle: real {rating} only.

## Supporting elements

- **Category donut card**: "Food {percent}% · Transport {percent}%".
- **Remaining chip**: "{amount} left this month" in the storefront currency.
- **Notifications**: "Electricity bill due tomorrow", "Dining is at {percent}% of budget", "{service} renews in 3 days".
- **Goal ring**: "Holiday · {saved} of {target}".
- **Transaction rows** with category icons and generic merchants ("Coffee shop", "Supermarket").
- **Security badges** (true ones only): "Read-only access", "Encrypted", "Face ID lock".
- **Comparison**: "Spreadsheet every Sunday" vs "Auto-categorised in real time".

## Visual direction

- Calm and trustworthy: greens, teals and navy (finance-pro, mint); red reserved strictly for over-budget states.
- Big tabular numerals for amounts (Inter, SF Pro Rounded for a friendlier feel); charts crisp, flat and few per shot.
- Sample data must look real but fictional; amounts and currency symbols match the locale.
- Leaders: Copilot and Monarch go sleek and dark-premium, YNAB friendly and educational, Spendee colourful; pick one tone and hold it.
- Lift key UI pieces (keypad, category chip, goal ring) out of the device so they're readable at thumbnail size.
- Avoid stress imagery (empty wallets, red arrows); show calm control.

## Avoid

- Outcome claims: "Save $500 a month", "Get out of debt fast" — need substantiation (Apple 2.3.1, FTC, Google Play Financial Services policy).
- Bank logos or "Connect to {bank}" for banks not actually supported; no implied partnerships (Apple 5.2.1).
- Real names, account numbers or recognisable merchant brands in sample data — use fictional, generic merchants.
- Security superlatives ("bank-level security", "unhackable") unless accurate and defensible.
- Prices, trials or "free forever" in screenshots (2.3.7).
- Debt or credit offers (loans, BNPL) — those pull in Google Play's personal-loan disclosure rules; keep them out unless the app is licensed for it.
- Conversion mistakes: dense spreadsheet screens, an onboarding/login screen as shot 1, mixed currencies, five charts in one shot, red-heavy palettes.
