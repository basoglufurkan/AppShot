# Investing & Crypto

## The searcher

- The first-time investor who wants to start small and is scared of doing it wrong — needs simplicity and trust more than charts.
- The active trader who wants speed, pro charts, indicators and alerts that fire on time.
- The crypto holder with coins on three exchanges and two wallets who just wants one honest portfolio number (trackers).
- The dividend/long-term investor who wants to see income and allocation, not noise.
- Savers protecting purchasing power across gold, FX and funds. After installing, everyone wants to feel: informed, in control, not gambling.

## What wins the install

1. **Everything in one honest view** — a clean portfolio overview (with sample data) answers "how am I doing?" in a glance.
2. **Trust & safety first** — licensing, custody, biometrics, 2FA: the deciding objection for any app that touches money.
3. **Simple first steps** (brokers) — how easy it is to place a first order; fractional/any-amount investing only if real.
4. **Pro-grade tools** (traders) — candlesticks, indicators, depth, fast execution UI.
5. **Alerts that matter** — price, earnings, dividend, big moves; never miss what you care about.
6. **Learn while you invest** — explainers and plain-language insights reduce fear.

- **Portfolio total**: "{portfolioValue}" with a modest sample change — never a dramatic gain.
- **Allocation donut**: Stocks · ETFs · Crypto · Cash.
- **Price alert notification**: "{ticker} crossed {price}" / "{ticker} reports earnings today".

- **Risk line**: a small "Capital at risk" footer where local rules expect it.

## Visual direction

- Dark mode dominates (midnight, finance-pro) with semantic green/red; crypto apps add a neon accent, minimalist brokers go mono.
- Tabular numerals everywhere (IBM Plex Sans/Mono, Inter with tnum); charts big, thin-lined, uncluttered.
- Trust beats hype: navy/teal and generous whitespace for brokers; avoid rockets, moons, lambos and money-rain imagery.
- Leaders (Robinhood-style minimal, eToro-social, Coinbase clean blue, TradingView pro-dark) show one asset or one portfolio per shot.
- Keep sample data realistic and boring — modest gains, diversified holdings.
- Lift the order ticket or alert card out of the device so the key UI is legible in search results.

## Avoid

- Apple 3.1.5: crypto wallets must come from developers enrolled as an organisation; exchanges only in licensed jurisdictions and offered by the exchange itself; no on-device mining; no crypto rewards for downloading apps or other tasks.
- Google Play Financial Services policy: crypto exchanges and wallets must meet each country's licensing requirements; binary options are banned outright.
- Return promises or hype: "guaranteed returns", "double your money", "risk-free", "to the moon", past performance as a promise — violates financial-promotion rules (SEC/FINRA, UK FCA crypto promotion rules) and Apple 2.3.1.
- Advice framing: "Buy now", "Top 5 stocks to buy" — reads as personalised investment advice.
- "Zero fees" / "commission-free" unless always true and fully disclosed; no prices or fees in screenshots (2.3.7).
- Real people's portfolios or giant sample gains (+340%).
- Exchange, coin or company logos implying partnership; ticker symbols as plain text are fine.
- Conversion mistakes: a cluttered pro terminal as shot 1 for a beginner app, unreadable micro-numbers, no security story.
