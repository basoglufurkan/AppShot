# Nutrition & Calorie Counter

## The searcher

- Someone who has decided to lose weight and was told to "track your calories". They search "calorie counter" and want it to be painless.
- A gym-goer hitting protein and macro targets who logs several times a day and hates slow apps.
- A low-carb or keto dieter tracking net carbs.
- A person on a weight-management medication journey who needs to keep protein and nutrition in check. It's a fast-growing intent.
- Biggest objection: "logging food is tedious and my food won't be in the database." What they want to feel: "I know what I'm eating, without effort."

## What wins the install

1. **Effortless logging.** AI photo scan, barcode scan or voice logging, shown fast. Tedium is the #1 objection and the #1 reason people churn.
2. **An instantly readable daily dashboard.** A calories-left ring plus macro bars tells the whole story in one glance.
3. **Database depth, including local food.** "{foods}+ foods, local brands too" kills "it won't have my food". Localize the demo foods per market.
4. **A personal target.** A calculated goal from age, weight and activity makes it feel tailored and credible.
5. **Progress you can see.** A weight trend and logging streak keep motivation alive.
6. **Recipes and meal plans as the upside,** not the headline.

## Supporting elements

- Calories-left ring: "{kcalLeft} kcal left", with Eaten {kcalEaten} and Burned {kcalBurned}.
- Macro bars: Protein {protein} g / Carbs {carbs} g / Fat {fat} g, each against its target.
- AI scan card: food photo with item labels like "Grilled chicken · {kcal} kcal" and a confidence-neutral "Edit" chip.
- Barcode frame with a generic product card. No real brand packaging.
- Meal timeline cards: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner, Snacks, each with kcal.
- Weight trend line with a dashed goal line, plus a streak chip "{streak} days logged".
- Comparison row: Type it in vs Snap it vs Scan it, with steps or seconds (measured only).
- Localize demo meals for each storefront.

## Visual direction

- Fresh and appetizing: mint, forest green or clean-light, with sunset accents for a warmer, friendlier brand.
- Real, colorful food photography (bowls, plates, fresh produce) breaking out of the device. Food is the emotional hook.
- Typography: rounded friendly sans with large clear numerals. Rings and bars must read at thumbnail size.
- Leaders use green palettes, ring dashboards, AI scan shots with bounding labels and diverse cuisines. Avoid sad diet imagery (tiny salads, measuring tapes).

## Avoid

- Weight-loss guarantees, rapid-loss claims and before/after body photos. Apple 1.4.1 / 2.3.1 and Google Play's health misinformation policy apply, and ad networks reject them too.
- Very low calorie targets in demo data (for example under 1,200 kcal). It's an eating-disorder sensitivity, and reviewers read it as irresponsible.
- Medical claims ("manage diabetes", "reverse insulin resistance", medication dosing) and weight-loss drug brand names (Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro). That combines trademark and medical-claim risk. Say "medication journey" only if the feature exists.
- "100% accurate" AI scan claims. Label AI estimates as editable.
- Food brand logos or packaging in barcode demos (Apple 2.3.9).
- Apple Health / Health Connect marks only per their brand guidelines. HealthKit data must never feed advertising (Apple 5.1.3).
- No subscription prices, trials or "#1 calorie app" claims. Ratings and user counts only from real data.
- Conversion killers: a food search list as shot 1, numbers too small to read, unappetizing food photos, and a dense nutrient table as the hero.
