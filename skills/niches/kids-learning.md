# Kids Learning

## The searcher

- The buyer is the parent, not the child: often searching at night or in a waiting room, slightly guilty about screen time and wanting it to "count".
- Triggered by a moment: a teacher mentions phonics, the child struggles with letters, kindergarten starts next autumn, a long flight is coming.
- Grandparents and relatives shopping for a specific age; homeschooling parents who want a curriculum, not a toy.
- Tablet-first: much of the usage is on iPad and Android tablets, so tablet screenshots get disproportionate attention.
- After installing they want to feel: "This is screen time I'm proud of — and my kid actually asks for it."

## What wins the install

1. **Parent-trust signals** — safe, ad-free, no external links, works offline; the #1 objection is "will this expose my child to ads or strangers?"
2. **A learning outcome parents can name** — "reads first words", "counts to 20", "knows letter sounds" beats "fun games".
3. **Visible delight** — characters and joyful moments signal the child will choose it without nagging.
4. **Age fit** — a clear age band ({ageRange}) stops the "too babyish / too hard" worry.
5. **Progress for parents** — a report or parent zone proves the minutes were productive.
6. **Credible design** — educators or curriculum alignment, only when true and nameable.

## Story (shot by shot)

1. **Hook** — learning through play — show: hero gameplay moment with a character and a letter or number — layout: full-bleed — angle: a child-sized win ("first word read") captioned for the parent.
2. **Safety** — peace of mind — show: parent zone or settings — layout: feature-list — angle: 3–4 true trust points (no ads, offline, parental gate, no chat).
3. **Skills** — what they'll learn — show: skill or world map (letters, numbers, shapes, logic) — layout: screen-grid — angle: the curriculum at a glance.
4. **Hands-on** — active, not passive video — show: letter tracing or counting activity — layout: zoom-callout — angle: zoom on the finger path and the star reward.
5. **Age fit** — grows with your child — show: age or level selection — layout: floating-cards — angle: {ageRange} chips around the device.
6. **Parent view** — see their progress — show: parent report — layout: hero-device — angle: "{childName} learned {letters} letters this week".
7. **Anywhere** — offline and profiles — show: offline badge plus multiple child profiles — layout: ui-pieces — angle: car, plane, grandma's house.

## Supporting elements

- **Trust badges** (only the true ones): "No ads", "No in-app chat", "Parental gate", "Works offline", "Teacher Approved" (Google Play, only if awarded).
- **Parent report card**: "{childName} practised {minutes} min · {letters} new letters".
- **Age chips**: "{ageRange}" or per-level chips (2–3, 4–5, 6–7).
- **Skill tiles**: Letters · Numbers · Shapes · Colours · Logic · Feelings.
- **Reward burst**: stars and stickers popping out of the frame around the activity.
- **Parent notification**: "Weekly progress report is ready".
- **Character speech bubble**: short, positive, in the child's language ("You did it!") — one per shot at most.

## Visual direction

- Bright primaries or soft pastels in a strongly illustrated world; thick rounded type (Baloo 2, Fredoka, Nunito Black) at very large sizes.
- Let characters break out of the device frame; full-bleed gameplay often beats a framed phone because the UI *is* the illustration.
- Make iPad/tablet sets first-class — parents browse on the device the child uses.
- Two-tone captions: a big playful headline plus a small, calm reassurance line for the parent ("No ads · Offline").
- Category leaders (Khan Academy Kids, Lingokids, Pok Pok) sell a *world*, not menus; never lead with settings or account screens.
- High contrast, no tiny text — parents decide in under two seconds.

## Avoid

- Apple 1.3 and 5.1.4 (Kids Category): no third-party ads or analytics, parental gate before links, purchases or permission prompts — screenshots must not show external links, purchase buttons or social sharing.
- Google Play Families Policy: the declared target audience must match the screenshots; only certified ad SDKs; the "Teacher Approved" badge only when Google actually awarded it.
- COPPA / GDPR-K: never show a real child's face, name or voice without written releases; never show sign-up or data-collection forms.
- Outcome claims parents can't verify: "raises IQ", "makes your child smarter", "proven to improve grades", "reading in 2 weeks".
- "Free" when most content sits behind a subscription (misleading under Apple 2.3.1); no prices or trial banners (2.3.7).
- Licensed characters (Peppa, Disney, Cocomelon) or look-alikes you don't own.
- Conversion mistakes: captions written for the child ("Let's play!") instead of the parent who pays, character art with no learning visible, dark or busy backgrounds, menus as shot 1.
