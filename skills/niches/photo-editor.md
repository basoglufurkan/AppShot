# Photo Editor

## The searcher

- A social poster who wants a consistent, recognisable look across their feed, usually through presets.
- A hobby photographer moving from desktop editing to mobile who needs RAW support and precise tools to take it seriously.
- A casual user fixing one photo: lighting too dark, a blemish, a crooked horizon.
- What they want to feel: "My photos finally look like the ones I admire," and "I'm in control of every detail."

## What wins the install

1. **A signature look in shot 1.** One beautifully graded photo sells the aesthetic, and the aesthetic is what people install. The tool list comes second.
2. **One-tap presets for casual users.** A carousel of looks applied to the same photo answers "I don't know how to edit".
3. **Proof of depth for serious users.** Curves, HSL, masks and RAW shown as real UI pieces. Pros scan for these exact words.
4. **Selective, local edits.** Masking sky or subject is now the line between toy and tool.
5. **Natural retouching.** Selfie users want fixes that still look like them.
6. **Uncompromised output.** Full-resolution export and non-destructive editing matter to the people who leave reviews.

## Supporting elements

- Slider stack card: Exposure +0.4, Contrast +12, Grain 20, the real values used for that edit.
- Tone curve panel cutout with R / G / B channel chips.
- HSL color dots with one channel selected.
- Preset chip row with the app's real preset names and thumbnails.
- Histogram overlay in a corner of the photo.
- Mask outline (sky or subject) glowing, with a "Sky selected" label.
- "Hold to compare" tooltip on the photo, and an edit history stack with the last few steps.

## Visual direction

- Photography leads and UI stays neutral. Mono, editorial and midnight let the image colors do the talking. Use clean-light for a friendlier, beginner-oriented editor.
- Thin elegant type (editorial serif or tight grotesk), small-caps labels, generous negative space. It should feel like a photo book, not a tech ad.
- Mix full-bleed photos with floating UI cutouts instead of eight identical full-device shots.
- Curate a coherent photo set (travel, portrait, food, street) graded consistently so the set itself demonstrates "a look".
- Category leaders are minimalist and photo-first, with sliders floating and diagonal splits. Garish over-saturated grades look dated and amateur.

## Avoid

- Competitor or platform names (Lightroom, VSCO, Instagram) in screenshot captions. This is an Apple 2.3.7 / 5.2.1 and Google Play metadata policy issue. Users still search them, so let the keyword field do that work.
- Unlicensed stock photos, or recognizable people without model releases (Apple 2.3.9).
- Edits that weren't made in the app. Every after-state should be reproducible with the shown tools (2.3.1).
- Aggressive body reshaping or skin-lightening examples. They draw beauty-standard backlash, and some ad networks reject them outright.
- Android UI or devices in iOS screenshots (Apple 2.3.10), and iOS chrome in Play screenshots.
- "Free presets", prices or sale banners in the images. No "#1 photo editor" without a citable source.
- Conversion killers: eight repeated full-device layouts, tiny photos inside UI, tool names listed without the visible result, and grades so strong they look like a filter app.
