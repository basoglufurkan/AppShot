# AI Photo Editor

## The searcher

- Has one specific broken photo right now (blurry, dark, an old print, a stranger in the background) and searches the fix itself: "unblur photo", "remove object", "enhance photo".
- A social poster who wants a glow-up without learning a pro editor.
- A small seller who needs clean product shots on white or styled AI backgrounds today.
- A curious creator who wants AI styles and art made from their own photos.
- What they want to feel: "One tap fixed it, and it still looks real."

## What wins the install

1. **A dramatic but believable before/after in shot 1.** The transformation is the product, and the difference has to be obvious at thumbnail size.
2. **One tap, no skills.** Show the single button and the result. That kills the "editing is hard" objection.
3. **One tool per search intent.** Enhance, erase, background, restore and expand are separate searches. Give each top tool its own shot so every searcher sees their problem solved.
4. **Realism.** Plastic skin and melted hands kill trust instantly. Pick outputs where faces still look like the person.
5. **Speed.** A visible "seconds" progress state makes the magic feel effortless.
6. **Output quality.** HD export, and "no watermark" only if that's true of the tier shown.

1. **Hook** — the #1 fix, solved — show: blurry or low-res portrait vs the enhanced result on the same subject — layout: before-after — angle: blurry to sharp in one tap.

## Visual direction

- Make the photo the hero. Use full-bleed images with a thin device or none at all. Dark backgrounds (midnight, neon) make colors pop, and aurora suits generative and creative positioning.
- The "before" side must be the genuine input and the "after" the genuine app output. Never fake the delta in a desktop editor.
- Use a bold condensed sans in white on dark, with one gradient accent for the highlight word and the magic button.
- Vary subjects across the set (portrait, pet, landscape, product, vintage family photo) so every searcher sees their kind of photo.
- Top apps run massive face before/after diagonals, sparkle accents on AI buttons and tool-by-tool carousels. Restraint stands out: one clean split per shot.

## Avoid

- Before/after pairs not produced by the app, or retouched afterwards. That's misleading under Apple 2.3.1 and Google Play's deceptive behavior policy, and users screenshot the mismatch in reviews.
- "Undress", "see through clothes", body-sexualizing or "make anyone…" marketing. It's explicitly prohibited under Google Play's AI-generated content and sexual content policies and Apple 1.1.4.
- Celebrity faces, or recognizable people without a model release (Apple 2.3.9, right of publicity). No celebrity face swaps.
- Styles named after studios, artists or franchises. Name them generically ("soft anime", "watercolor").
- Generated images presented as real photographs of events. Disclose AI generation, and on Play offer in-app reporting of offensive output.
- "No watermark", "unlimited", "free", credit counts or prices in screenshots unless they're true for what's shown. Prices never.
- No invented ratings or "#1 photo app" claims.
- Conversion killers: a before/after delta too subtle at thumbnail size, one tool repeated across five shots, and a small device frame that shrinks the photo to a stamp.
