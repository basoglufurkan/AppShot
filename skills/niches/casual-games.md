# Casual Games

## The searcher

- Bored in a queue, on the metro or on the sofa — wants something satisfying within seconds, no tutorial wall.
- Unwinding before bed: relaxing puzzles, no timers, no pressure.
- The 35+ brain-game player (word, sudoku, solitaire, mahjong) who wants to "keep the mind sharp" and enjoys daily rituals.
- Merge/decor fans who love progression, renovation and light story.
- Flight and commute players searching "offline games". After installing they want to feel: satisfied, relaxed, a little clever — and pulled into "one more level".

## What wins the install

1. **The mechanic, understood in one second** — the viewer must grasp how it plays from a single frame of real gameplay.
2. **Satisfaction** — the juicy moment (line clear, combo, merge, word found) is what people install for.
3. **Relaxation / no pressure** — no timers, no Wi-Fi needed, play at your pace (only if true).
4. **Mental workout** — "exercise your mind" framing for word/number games, without medical claims.
5. **Progression and variety** — worlds, themes, decorations, a story to unlock.
6. **Daily reasons to return** — daily challenges, events, collections.

## Supporting elements

- **Combo burst**: "x{combo}" or "Perfect!" exactly as the game shows it.
- **Level badge**: "Level {level}" with stars earned.
- **Daily challenge calendar** with today's trophy.
- **Booster icons** (only those in the game) floating around the board.
- **"No Wi-Fi needed"** and **"No timers"** pills — only if true.
- **Character speech bubble** for story-driven games ("Help me fix the garden!").
- **Collection progress**: "{collected}/{total} items".

## Visual direction

- Full-bleed gameplay, no device frame for most games — the frame shrinks what sells; match the game's orientation (portrait or landscape sets).
- Saturated, high-contrast colour (playful, neon, sunset, pastel); chunky rounded or bold display type with outline or drop shadow so captions pop on busy art.
- Captions short (2–4 words) and placed where they don't hide the mechanic.
- Leaders (Royal Match, Block Blast, Wordscapes, Microsoft Solitaire, Merge Mansion) show real boards at the most satisfying instant, plus characters or decor for meta.
- Keep the UI honest: real HUD, real boosters, real art — no pre-rendered scenes that aren't in the game.
- Calm puzzle and brain games use softer palettes (pastel, cream) and elegant type to signal "relaxing".

## Avoid

- Misleading gameplay — mechanics, levels or art not in the game (the "fake ad" pin-pull problem) breaks Apple 2.3.3/2.3.4 and Google Play's misleading-metadata rules, and tanks retention.
- Brain-training health claims ("prevents dementia", "boosts IQ", "clinically proven") — the FTC fined Lumosity's maker for exactly this in 2016.
- Loot boxes without disclosed odds (Apple 3.1.1; Google Play requires odds disclosure for randomised items); "win real money" or cash-prize framing (real-money gaming rules).
- Prices of coins, gems or bundles, and "free" banners (2.3.7).
- Unlicensed characters, brands or look-alike art.
- Kid-appealing visuals without matching Families policy compliance if children are a target audience.
- Conversion mistakes: tiny boards inside a device frame, shop and ad buttons cluttering the HUD, long captions, five different art styles in one set.
