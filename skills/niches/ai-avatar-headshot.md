# AI Avatar & Headshot

## The searcher

- A job seeker or professional who needs a profile or CV photo this week and doesn't want to book a photographer. They search "ai headshot" or "professional photo".
- A social user who wants a fun pack (anime, fantasy, yearbook trend) to post and share.
- Someone refreshing a dating or creator profile who wants polished portraits that still look like them.
- Their biggest doubt is "Will it actually look like me?", followed by "What happens to my face photos?"
- What they want to feel: "That's me on my best day."

## What wins the install

1. **Likeness proof.** Casual selfies next to the outputs of the same person answer the #1 objection. Without it, gorgeous outputs read as stock photos.
2. **Volume and variety in a grid.** Many outfits, backdrops and lighting setups signal value before the user reads anything.
3. **One clear use case per audience.** Pro (profile, CV, team page) or fun (styles, trends). Mixing both in shot 1 weakens each.
4. **A short process.** "Upload {minPhotos} selfies → pick a style → get your photos" in three steps makes it feel safe and quick.
5. **Privacy.** Visible face-data handling (deletion window, no training on your photos, if true) decides pro users.
6. **Natural quality.** Real skin texture and correct hands beat over-smoothed perfection.

## Story (shot by shot)

1. **Hook** — the outcome — show: a 3×3 grid of polished headshots of one consenting model, with a small inset of their casual selfies — layout: screen-grid — angle: studio headshots from your selfies.
2. **Likeness** — still you — show: one casual selfie beside one finished headshot of the same person — layout: before-after — angle: still you, just polished.
3. **Styles** — choice — show: the same person in office, outdoor and studio looks on fanned devices — layout: trio-fan — angle: pick your outfit and backdrop.
4. **How it works** — effortless — show: upload screen, style picker, results ready — layout: feature-list — angle: three steps, no photographer.
5. **In context** — the payoff — show: the headshot on a generic profile card (name, title placeholders) — layout: hero-device — angle: ready for your profile and CV.
6. **Privacy and quality** — close objections — show: HD download and data controls — layout: full-bleed — angle: your selfies stay private (only if true).

For fun avatar packs, swap shot 2 for a style-pack grid and open with the most viral pack.

## Supporting elements

- Input stack: 3–4 small casual selfies labeled "{minPhotos}+ selfies", with an arrow to a finished portrait.
- Style tags: Corporate, Studio, Outdoor, Creative. Background swatch row: grey, navy, office, city.
- Pack card: "{photos} photos · {styles} styles · HD", matching the real pack contents.
- Ready notification: "Your headshots are ready", in the app's real push copy.
- Privacy chip: "Photos deleted after {days} days", only if the policy says so.
- Generic profile card mockup (avatar, "Name Surname", "Product Designer") showing the headshot in use.

## Visual direction

- Pro positioning: clean-light, editorial or luxe. Neutral greys, navy and warm off-white, soft studio light, refined serif or tight grotesk captions.
- Fun positioning: aurora gradients with stylized pack covers and playful type.
- Make faces large. Grids work best without a device frame, so use a device only for the process and profile shots.
- Keep each comparison to one person, and vary people across the set (age, skin tone, gender) so more searchers see themselves.
- Top apps use masonry grids of 9–12 headshots with a small input-selfie inset and "profile-ready" framing, without any platform logos.

## Avoid

- Real customers' outputs without explicit written consent. Use a model-released demo person (Apple 2.3.9).
- LinkedIn's logo or UI in screenshots. That's a trademark issue. Say "profile" or "CV" in captions.
- Outputs that change ethnicity, age, body shape or facial features. It's misleading against real results (2.3.1) and a reputational risk.
- Sexualized avatar packs, minors' photos, or encouraging uploads of other people's faces. This trips Apple 1.1.4 and Google Play's child safety and AI-generated content policies. State 18+ and own-photos-only in the app.
- Face-data promises ("deleted in 24h", "never used for training") that differ from the privacy policy, App Privacy labels or Play Data safety form.
- Calling outputs "real photos" or implying a photographer took them. Disclose AI generation.
- Pack prices, credits, "free" or discount badges in screenshots. No invented "{users} headshots created" numbers.
- Conversion killers: showing only one output, outputs that visibly don't match the input selfie, and tiny thumbnails inside a device frame.
