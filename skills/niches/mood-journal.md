# Mood Tracker & Journal

## The searcher

- Someone who wants to understand their ups and downs ("why do I feel like this on Sundays?"). They search "mood tracker".
- A would-be journaler who has abandoned blank notebooks and needs prompts or a 30-second format.
- A gratitude or self-reflection seeker inspired by social media or a book.
- A privacy-conscious writer who wants a diary only they can open.
- What they want to feel: "A tiny daily check-in that slowly reveals patterns about me."

## What wins the install

1. **Check-in speed.** Tap a mood and a few activities in seconds. That kills the "blank page / no time" objection.
2. **Pattern reveal.** A colorful mood calendar, year-in-pixels and correlations ("you feel better on days you walk") are the curiosity hook.
3. **Prompts.** Guided questions make journaling easy for people who don't know what to write.
4. **Privacy lock.** A diary has to feel safe. Lock and backup are decisive for this audience.
5. **Beauty and personality.** Aesthetic themes, stickers and photos make it feel like their own notebook.
6. **Memories.** "On this day" resurfacing adds emotional value over time.

## Supporting elements

- Mood picker row: five faces or colors with labels (Awful → Great).
- Activity tags: Sleep, Friends, Exercise, Work, Family, Nature.
- Insight card: "You felt better on days with {activity}", from the demo account's own entries.
- Year-in-pixels grid with a color legend.
- Prompt card: "What made you smile today?" or "What do you need right now?".
- Lock badge: passcode or biometric lock icon (Face ID named only per Apple's guidelines).
- Streak chip and an "On this day, {years} year ago" memory card.

## Visual direction

- Pastel, cream, calm and sunset palettes: soft illustrations, paper textures, handwritten accents, washi-tape details.
- Typography: rounded sans plus a handwritten or script accent for prompts and entries.
- Colorful mood data is the hero. Calendars and pixel grids pop on soft backgrounds.
- Leaders use colorful grids, cute characters, journal-page aesthetics and lock icons. A wall of entry text reads like homework.

## Avoid

- Diagnostic language ("track your depression", "bipolar test", "anxiety score") unless clinically validated. Apple 1.4.1 and Google Play's health policy apply. Keep the framing to wellbeing and self-reflection.
- Real personal entries from users. Write warm, believable demo entries.
- "End-to-end encrypted" or "100% private" claims unless technically true and matched by the privacy labels and Play Data safety form.
- AI insights presented as professional or clinical advice.
- Prices, trial text or invented "{users} journalers" counts.
- Conversion killers: walls of text entries, dull grey charts, a blank editor as the hook, and a missing lock/privacy shot.
