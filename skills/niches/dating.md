# Dating

## The searcher

- A single person tired of the big apps (swipe fatigue, ghosting, fake profiles) who is looking for something more intentional or simply different.
- Someone from a specific community (faith, culture, age group, LGBTQ+, profession, location) looking for an app where "their people" are.
- A newcomer to a city who wants to meet people and date locally.
- What they want to feel: "Real people, real chemistry, and safe while I find it."

## What wins the install

1. **Differentiation, immediately.** Apple's 4.3 guideline lists dating among saturated categories, and searchers have seen every swipe clone. The reason this app is different (intent, community, mechanic) must be visible in shots 1–2.
2. **Real, verified people.** Fake profiles and catfishing are the top objection. Verification badges and genuine-looking profiles answer it.
3. **Conversation starters.** Prompts, voice notes and icebreakers kill "matches never talk".
4. **Quality over quantity.** "Fewer, better matches" positioning wins with burned-out daters.
5. **Visible safety.** Report, block, a safety center and date check-ins. Women in particular look for this before installing.
6. **Inclusivity.** Seeing yourself in the screenshots (age, body, ethnicity, orientation) is a strong install signal.

## Supporting elements

- Profile card: photo, first name and age, a prompt answer ("My ideal Sunday is…") and a verified badge.
- Mutual-like moment: "You and {name} liked each other" with a soft celebration.
- Icebreaker chat bubbles: a playful opener and a warm reply.
- Voice prompt card with waveform and play button.
- Shared-interest chips: Hiking, Coffee, Live music, Dogs.
- Safety card: Report · Block · Share date details · Safety tips.
- Filter chips: Age {ageRange}, Distance {distance} km, Looking for: Long-term.

## Avoid

- Sexual or suggestive imagery and copy ("hookup tonight", "NSFW", lingerie or bed shots). Apple 2.3.8 requires metadata appropriate for all audiences even if the app is rated 18+, and Apple 1.1.4 and Google Play's sexual content policy apply. Play prohibits compensated-relationship ("sugar") apps.
- Real users' photos or profiles without explicit consent. Use licensed models with releases (Apple 2.3.9).
- Screenshots implying unmoderated chat. User-generated content apps need filtering, reporting, blocking and contact info (Apple 1.2), and Play's child safety standards policy requires social and dating apps to publish CSAE standards.
- Invented "{users} singles near you", fake match counts or fabricated success stories.
- Anyone who looks under 18. Adult-only models, clearly.
- Generic swipe-clone screenshots. They invite 4.3 spam rejection and don't convert.
- Premium tiers with prices, "free" or "boost" pricing in screenshots.
- Conversion killers: every model looking the same, no visible differentiator, and tiny faces inside full device frames.
