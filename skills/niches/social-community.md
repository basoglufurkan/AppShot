# Social & Community

## The searcher

- Someone looking for "their people" around an interest, identity, life stage or place, and tired of noisy, algorithm-driven mega-networks.
- A creator or hobbyist who wants a niche audience that actually engages.
- A newcomer to a city, hobby or life phase (new parents, expats, students) who wants groups and real-life events.
- What they want to feel: "I belong here, the conversations are worth it, and people are kind."

## What wins the install

1. **Belonging, specifically.** Show the exact community vibe and topic. "Where {interest} fans talk" beats "connect with friends" every time.
2. **Proof of life.** A feed and threads full of believable activity answer the cold-start fear that "nobody's here".
3. **A visible differentiator.** Chronological feed, no ads, local focus, interest-first or creator tools. Say why it isn't just another network.
4. **Kindness and safety.** Moderation and community guidelines matter to users, and they're required for user-generated content apps.
5. **Real-life connection.** Events and meetups turn online belonging into something tangible.
6. **Momentum.** Notifications of replies and invites show the app is social, not a ghost town.

## Story (shot by shot)

1. **Hook** — belonging — show: a vibrant community feed with posts, images and reactions from the target interest — layout: hero-device — angle: find your people.
2. **Conversations** — quality — show: a comment thread or group chat with warm, on-topic replies — layout: chat-bubbles — angle: conversations worth joining.

## Supporting elements

- Post card: avatar, name, a short on-topic post, image and reaction row with plausible demo counts.
- Comment thread bubbles with nested replies and a "Reply" affordance.
- Notification stack: "{name} replied to your post", "You're invited to {group}", "{event} starts tomorrow".
- Group cards: cover image, name and "{members} members" (real counts only).
- Event card: "Sat · {place} · {going} going" with avatar faces.
- Moderation chip: "Kindness first: report or block anytime".
- Interest tag chips for the community's real topics.

## Visual direction

- Aurora, playful and neon for Gen Z and creator communities. Clean-light for local, professional or parent communities. Content-rich, colorful screenshots.
- Avatars: illustrated or licensed photos. Demo posts must be written for the screenshot, never taken from real users.
- Large chat bubbles and post cards, and the trio-fan to show the product's surfaces.
- Leaders use vivid feeds, stickers and reactions, notification stacks and event cards. Empty states and generic stock photos signal a ghost town.

## Avoid

- Missing UGC safeguards. Apple 1.2 requires filtering, reporting, blocking and published contact info, and "Chatroulette-style" random or anonymous chat screenshots invite rejection.
- For any app open to teens, Google Play's child safety standards policy requires published CSAE standards, and age-appropriate design applies.
- Real users' posts, photos or names without consent. No celebrity content or other networks' logos and UI.
- Fabricated activity: inflated member counts or thousands of fake reactions (Apple 2.3.1, Play deceptive behavior). Demo numbers stay modest and plausible, and stated counts must be real.
- Political, offensive or controversial demo posts. They polarize viewers and draw reviewer attention.
- Prices, premium badges with pricing, or "free" claims.
- Conversion killers: empty or sparse feeds, generic "connect with friends" with no niche, and text too small to read the posts.
