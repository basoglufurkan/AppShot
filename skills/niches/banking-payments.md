# Banking & Payments

## The searcher

- Fed up with a slow, clunky bank app and branch queues — wants banking that feels like a modern app.
- An expat, student abroad or traveller sending money home or spending in another currency — cares about speed and seeing the fee upfront.
- A freelancer paid from abroad who needs an IBAN and a fast way to receive and convert.
- A young user who wants a card *now* (virtual card in minutes) and instant notifications for every payment.
- Friends splitting dinner and rent. After installing everyone wants to feel: fast, in control, safe.

## What wins the install

1. **Speed you can see** — send money in seconds, open an account in minutes (if true): show the actual flow.
2. **The card as a hero object** — a beautiful physical/virtual card is an aspiration and an identity signal.
3. **Control & security** — freeze/unfreeze, limits, instant notifications, biometrics: kills "is my money safe here?"
4. **Transparency** — "see the fee before you send" (without numbers) addresses the hidden-fees fear.
5. **Multi-currency / travel** — hold and spend currencies like a local.
6. **Social payments** — split, request, pay friends by link or QR.

## Story (shot by shot)

1. **Hook** — banking at your speed — show: home with balance and the card — layout: tilted-device — angle: glossy card floating in front of a tilted phone.
2. **Send** — money moves in seconds — show: amount pad → recipient → "Sent" confirmation — layout: ui-pieces — angle: three UI pieces in sequence.

## Visual direction

- One bold brand colour plus lots of white; the card is the hero object (glossy, 3D, sometimes metal for premium tiers — luxe).
- Big tabular numerals for balances; generous spacing; payment flows shown as clean, separate UI pieces.
- Trust palette (navy, teal, deep green) for traditional banks; vivid coral, purple or volt for neobanks and wallets.
- Leaders (Revolut, Monzo, N26, Wise, Papara, Enpara) keep every shot to one action and one number.
- Dark mode works for premium/metal tiers; light mode reads more trustworthy for mainstream banking.
- No generic stock photos of people holding phones; show the product.

## Avoid

- Calling a non-bank a "bank": in the US, UK, EU and Turkey the word is restricted to licensed banks; fintechs write "Banking services provided by {partnerBank}". Deposit-insurance claims (FDIC, FSCS, TMSF) only exactly as they apply.
- Google Play Financial Services policy: declare financial features; personal loans need APR and term disclosures, and loans requiring full repayment in 60 days or less are banned.
- Fees, rates or "free transfers" in screenshots — they change and can mislead (Apple 2.3.1, 2.3.7).
- Apple Pay / Google Pay / Visa / Mastercard marks outside their brand guidelines; no implied endorsement (Apple 5.2.1).
- Real or valid-looking card numbers, IBANs or customer names — always masked, fictional data.
- Security absolutes ("unhackable", "100% safe").
- Conversion mistakes: a login or PIN screen as shot 1, security walls of text, eight features crammed into one shot, a card that looks like a template.
