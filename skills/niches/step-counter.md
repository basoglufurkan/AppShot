# Step Counter

## The searcher

- Someone who wants to move more in the simplest possible way, with no workouts, gear or plans. They search "step counter" or "pedometer".
- A walker aiming to lose weight or feel healthier who wants a daily goal to chase.
- An older adult who wants a big, simple number and nothing else.
- A competitive type who wants challenges with friends or coworkers.
- What they want to feel: "My steps add up, and hitting my goal feels great."

## What wins the install

1. **One big number.** Today's steps against the goal is understood instantly. It's the whole product in one shot.
2. **Automatic and effortless.** Counts in the pocket, without GPS (if true) and light on battery. "Do I have to do anything?" No.
3. **Goal celebration.** Badges, streaks and the satisfaction of a completed ring keep people walking.
4. **Challenges and plans.** Friends, weekly challenges or walking plans add motivation beyond the number.
5. **Glanceable widgets.** Seeing steps on the home screen drives daily engagement.
6. **Health sync.** Fits into Apple Health / Health Connect for people who already track.

## Story (shot by shot)

1. **Hook** — the number — show: a giant step count ring "{steps} / {goal}" — layout: big-number — angle: every step counts.

## Supporting elements

- Giant ring or arc: "{steps}" with "Goal {goal}" and percent complete.
- Stat row: {distance} km · {kcal} kcal (labeled estimate) · {minutes} active min.
- Hourly bar chart with the peak walking hour highlighted.
- Badge cards: "First {goal}-step day", "{streak}-day streak", "Marathon distance this month".
- Notifications: "Goal reached: {goal} steps", "Just {stepsLeft} steps to go".
- Home-screen widget mockup with the ring.
- Leaderboard card with demo first names and a "You" row highlighted.

## Visual direction

- Bright, upbeat palettes: volt for sporty, mint for healthy and fresh, sunset for warm motivation, playful for casual or older audiences.
- Huge rounded numerals. Short, readable captions, since older users are a large share.
- Outdoor walking lifestyle photos (parks, city streets, dogs) with diverse ages, used sparingly behind the device.
- Leaders use one giant ring or number, colorful badge collections and home-screen widgets. The niche rewards simplicity, and 5–6 shots is enough.

## Avoid

- Exact calorie burn or weight-loss promises ("walk off 5 kg"). Label calories as estimates. Apple 1.4.1 / 2.3.1 and Google Play's health misinformation policy apply.
- "Walk to earn" or cash, crypto and gift-card reward claims unless real, disclosed and compliant. Google Play's real-money reward and financial services rules and Apple 3.1/3.2 scrutiny apply. Never show earnings amounts as bait.
- Copying Apple's activity-ring visual language exactly, or using Apple Watch / Apple Health marks outside their guidelines (Apple 4.1, 5.2.1).
- "Most accurate pedometer" or battery claims without measurement.
- Real users' names or photos on leaderboards. Use demo names.
- Prices, "remove ads" or trial text. No invented "{users} walkers" counts.
- Conversion killers: tiny numbers, gym imagery for a walking app, and too many shots for a simple, low-consideration category.
