# Translator

## The searcher

- A traveller abroad staring at a menu, a sign or a train ticket machine — needs to understand *now*, often without data.
- An expat or new arrival dealing with landlords, doctors, school letters and paperwork.
- A business traveller in meetings; hotel, shop and taxi staff in tourist areas (common in Turkey) talking to visitors in Arabic, Russian, German or English.
- Someone in a cross-language relationship or family chatting every day.
- After installing they want to feel: understood, confident and connected — the language barrier gone.

## What wins the install

1. **Point the camera, read the world** — live camera/AR translation over a real menu or sign is the visual magic.
2. **Face-to-face conversation** — split-screen two-way voice translation lets two people simply talk.
3. **Works offline** — downloadable language packs for roaming-free travel.
4. **Natural, context-aware results** — AI translations that sound human, not word-for-word.
5. **Breadth** — many languages, named, especially the storefront's top pairs.
6. **Hands-free and quick** — voice input, auto-detect, saved phrases, widgets.

## Story (shot by shot)

1. **Hook** — read any menu instantly — show: camera view with a foreign menu overlaid in the user's language — layout: split-diagonal — angle: original on one side, translated overlay on the other.
2. **Talk** — speak, and be understood — show: conversation mode with two languages — layout: chat-bubbles — angle: alternating bubbles (e.g. Turkish ⇄ German) with flags.
3. **Voice** — just speak — show: voice input with waveform and result — layout: hero-device — angle: big mic button, clean result card.
4. **Offline** — no Wi-Fi, still works — show: offline language packs — layout: floating-cards — angle: downloaded-pack chips around the device.
5. **Documents** — letters, photos, PDFs — show: translated document/photo — layout: zoom-callout — angle: zoom on a translated paragraph.
6. **Languages** (optional) — show: language picker — layout: feature-list — angle: top pairs named + "{languages} languages".

## Supporting elements

- **AR overlay**: a real menu (photographed by the team) with translated lines overlaid in place.
- **Conversation bubbles**: "Tren istasyonu nerede?" ⇄ "Wo ist der Bahnhof?" — every sample verified by native speakers.
- **Language-pair chip**: "Turkish ⇄ German" with an auto-detect indicator.
- **Offline pack chip**: "German · Downloaded · {size} MB".
- **Waveform + mic** for voice input.
- **Saved phrase cards**: "I'm allergic to nuts", "One ticket, please".
- **Flags row** with language names — not flags alone.

## Visual direction

- Clean and trustworthy: sky blue, white and glass panels; ocean for a calmer premium feel.
- The split-diagonal or before/after between two languages is the signature visual.
- Real-world context photos (menus, street signs, station boards) shot by the team; translated overlays crisp and legible.
- Leaders: Google Translate (utility), DeepL (minimal navy, "natural" positioning), iTranslate and SayHi (conversation-first).
- Pick language pairs for each storefront (TR store: Turkish ⇄ English/German/Arabic/Russian; US store: English ⇄ Spanish/French/Japanese).
- Keep bubbles large and short; two languages per shot maximum.

## Avoid

- Wrong sample translations — native speakers spot them instantly and leave reviews; verify every string.
- "100% accurate", "certified" or "sworn translation" (yeminli tercüme) claims unless a certified human service exists (Apple 2.3.1).
- Offline claims for languages that aren't actually available offline.
- Competitor names in captions ("Better than Google Translate") — Apple 2.3.7, trademarks.
- Medical or legal reliance framing ("translate your diagnosis") without caveats.
- Prices or "free" when camera/offline modes are premium (2.3.7).
- Conversion mistakes: an empty text box as the hook, no real-world context, five languages in one shot, flags without names.
