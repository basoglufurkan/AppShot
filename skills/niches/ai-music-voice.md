# AI Music & Voice

## The searcher

- A non-musician who wants a song for someone: a birthday, an anniversary, a friend's roast. They search "make a song with ai" or "song with name".
- A creator who needs voiceovers or background music for videos without recording themselves.
- A gamer, streamer or prankster looking for a voice changer with fun effects.
- A singer or karaoke fan who wants vocals removed from a track.
- What they want to feel: "I made that, and it actually sounds good."

This niche covers several intents. Lead the set with the app's primary one (song generation, voice tools or vocal separation) and keep the others for later shots.

## What wins the install

1. **Make sound visible.** Screenshots are silent, so sell audio with waveforms, play states, album-style cover art and lyric sheets. A screen of form fields sells nothing.
2. **The personal song as a gift.** "A song for {name}" is the highest-emotion, highest-intent use case in consumer AI music.
3. **No skills required.** Describe it, pick a genre, get a full track with vocals. That removes "I'm not musical".
4. **Breadth of genres and voices.** Chips and covers across styles show range at a glance.
5. **Usage clarity for creators.** "Use it in your videos" matters, but only when the license grants it.
6. **Let the preview video do the listening.** The screenshots should make people want to press play.

## Supporting elements

- Now-playing card: cover art, "{trackTitle}", genre tag, duration and a scrub bar mid-play.
- Lyric sheet card with Verse / Chorus labels, using lyrics the app actually generated.
- Prompt bubble: "A {genre} song about {topic}", with an arrow to the cover art.
- Genre and mood chips: Pop, Rap, Lo-fi, Acoustic / Happy, Romantic, Epic.
- Voice picker cards: abstract avatar plus a synthetic voice name. Never a real person.
- Equalizer bars or a glowing waveform strip across the bottom of the canvas.
- Share chip: "Saved as ringtone" / "Shared to stories".
- Rights badge: "Use in your videos", only if the license grants it.

## Visual direction

- Dark and saturated (neon, midnight) with glowing waveforms. Use sunset gradients for gift and love-song positioning.
- Square album-style covers floating out of the device read as "real music" instantly.
- Typography: a bold rounded display sans for captions, with lyrics set in a contrasting serif or handwritten style.
- Use play buttons, progress bars and equalizers on every shot. The set should feel like it's making sound.
- Top apps use streaming-app aesthetics: cover grids, big waveforms, a glowing Create button. Voice-changer apps lean playful with character avatars.

## Avoid

- Clones or "sound like {artist}" covers of real singers and celebrities. Voice-likeness laws (for example Tennessee's ELVIS Act), Apple 5.2.1 and Google Play's IP and impersonation policies all apply.
- Real song titles, lyrics, album art or label logos in demo content (Apple 2.3.9).
- "Royalty-free" or "commercial use" claims your license doesn't grant. Music platforms will Content ID-flag users' uploads, and the 1-star reviews follow.
- Cloning other people's voices without a consent flow, and marketing prank calls or impersonation. Disclose AI audio, and on Play provide in-app reporting for offensive output.
- Spotify, Apple Music or TikTok logos implying distribution or partnership.
- Credit packs, prices, "unlimited songs" or "free" in screenshots.
- Conversion killers: silent-looking shots with no waveform or play state, and giving song generation, voice changer and karaoke equal weight. Lead with the intent people search for most.
