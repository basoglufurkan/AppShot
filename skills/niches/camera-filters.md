# Camera & Filters

## The searcher

- A Gen Z or millennial user chasing the film / disposable / Y2K aesthetic without buying a real camera. They're trend-driven and search "film camera", "disposable camera", "vintage filter".
- A creator who wants a consistent vibe for stories and photo dumps.
- Someone nostalgic for date stamps, flash portraits and light leaks.
- What they want to feel: "My photos feel like memories, not phone pictures."

## What wins the install

1. **Mood over features.** The aesthetic is the product, so full-bleed photos with real grain and warmth sell before any UI does.
2. **The camera itself.** A charming skeuomorphic camera body or viewfinder separates "camera app" from "filter pack" and is very screenshot-friendly.
3. **Variety of looks.** A grid of distinct film and camera styles shows value without words.
4. **See it before you shoot.** Live filters in the viewfinder are a key differentiator from post-editing apps.
5. **Authentic details.** Grain, light leaks, dust and date stamps done well are what fans scrutinize.
6. **Ritual.** Roll counters and "developing" moments create delight and retention.

## Supporting elements

- Orange seven-segment date stamp in the photo corner. Use a stylised date and keep it consistent across shots.
- Film roll counter: "{shotsLeft}/{rollSize}" with a tiny canister icon.
- Camera picker carousel with small illustrated camera bodies named generically.
- Light leak and dust swatches, and a grain intensity slider.
- Instant-print frame cards with handwritten captions, scattered like a scrapbook.
- Macro shutter button detail and a flash on/off toggle chip.

## Visual direction

- Warm cream, editorial and pastel palettes: film borders, sprocket holes, tape, paper textures, handwritten notes. Mono works for a black-and-white or "pro analog" positioning.
- Typography: retro serif or monospaced date-stamp type for captions, and handwriting for small notes. Avoid sleek tech sans.
- Photos are huge and the device often secondary. A beautifully rendered skeuomorphic camera body can replace the phone frame in one shot.
- Subjects: friends laughing, golden hour, beach, night flash portraits, pets. Lifestyle shots with model consent, diverse and candid.
- Leaders use scrapbook collages of prints, camera picker grids and flash-lit party moments. Sterile UI-only shots underperform badly here.

## Avoid

- Real film stock and camera brand names (Kodak, Fujifilm, Portra, Polaroid, Instax, Leica) in captions, filter names or screenshots. It's a trademark issue (Apple 5.2.1, Play IP policy). Describe the look instead ("warm 400", "instant frame").
- Recognizable people without model releases (Apple 2.3.9).
- Before/after shots that don't use the app's actual filter output, or that were finished in another editor.
- Live-preview claims if the filter is only applied after capture.
- Prices, "free filters", or a "premium camera" badge with pricing.
- No invented download counts or "{users} creators" figures.
- Conversion killers: UI-only shots with no photos, dozens of tiny thumbnails with no hero mood, and generic captions like "Cool filters".
