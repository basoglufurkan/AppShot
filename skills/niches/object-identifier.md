# Object Identifier (Coins, Rocks, Insects & More)

## The searcher

- Covers single-category apps (coin, rock/crystal, insect, mushroom, bird, wine, antique ID) — keep the story on the category the app really serves.
- The finder: an old coin in a drawer, an inherited collection, a pretty stone from the beach — "what is it, and is it worth anything?"
- The curious parent or kid: "what bug is this?" on a garden walk.
- The collector building and valuing a collection; the flea-market or antique hunter checking before buying.
- After installing they want to feel: curiosity answered — and maybe the small thrill of a treasure.

## What wins the install

1. **Instant identification** — photo → named result with key facts is the magic moment.
2. **"What's it worth?"** (coins, antiques, minerals) — an estimated value range is the strongest hook, framed honestly as an estimate.
3. **Rich detail** — year, mint mark, composition, origin, rarity: the depth that makes it feel expert.
4. **Collection building** — catalogue finds, track total estimated value, show off.
5. **Safety info** (insects, spiders, mushrooms) — venomous/poisonous flags, framed as caution, never as clearance.
6. **Learning** — the story behind each find.

## Supporting elements

- **Result card**: "{itemName} · {confidence}% match" with 3 key facts.
- **Value card**: "Est. {valueLow}–{valueHigh}" labelled "estimate" — from the app's real data, never an extreme outlier.
- **Rarity badge**: Common · Scarce · Rare.
- **Feature chips**: Year · Mint · Composition · Origin (or Hardness · Lustre for minerals).
- **Collection total**: "{items} items · est. {totalValue}".
- **Safety badge** (insects/mushrooms): "Venomous — do not handle" / "Possibly toxic".
- **Scan reticle** over the object with corner brackets.

## Visual direction

- Match the material: coins on dark velvet with gold accents (luxe, midnight); minerals glowing on black; insects and mushrooms in forest greens; antiques and wine in cream/editorial.
- Macro photography of real objects shot by the team — texture and detail are the product.
- Elegant serif headlines for collectors (catalogue feel); sharp sans for scan UI.
- Leaders: CoinSnap, Rock Identifier, Picture Insect, Vivino, Merlin Bird ID — camera-first, result-card second, collection third.
- Keep value numbers modest and realistic; let rarity and story carry the excitement.
- One object per shot; clean background, strong lighting.

## Avoid

- Mushroom/insect safety framed as clearance ("Is it edible? Find out") — poisoning and bite risk; Apple 1.4.1. Always "never eat or handle based on an app".
- Value hype: "Your coin could be worth thousands!", extreme rare-coin values as typical — misleading under Apple 2.3.1; say "estimate", never "appraisal" or "certified".
- Apple 4.3 (spam): identifier apps are among the most cloned; screenshots must show genuine differentiation.
- Antiquities: many countries tightly restrict trading archaeological items — never promote selling ancient coins or artefacts.
- Wine scanners: alcohol content needs the right age rating; some markets restrict alcohol promotion — keep screenshots informational, no drinking imagery.
- Prices or "free" when scans/values are paywalled (2.3.7).
- Conversion mistakes: a category menu as the hook, generic magnifying-glass icons, blurry phone photos as "before", tiny facts text.
