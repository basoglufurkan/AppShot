# Books & Audiobooks

## The searcher

- The "I used to read so much" adult who wants books back in a life full of commutes, kids and screens.
- The commuter, runner or dish-washer who has ears free but not hands — audiobooks turn dead time into "reading".
- The self-improvement reader who wants the key ideas of a non-fiction book in 15 minutes.
- Genre bingers (romance, fantasy, web novels) who devour series and want the next chapter now.
- After installing they want to feel: "I'm a reader again — and it fits my day."

## What wins the install

1. **The catalogue feels deep and relevant** — covers sell books; a wall of real, licensed covers beats any feature list.
2. **Reading fits into life** — listening on the go and switching between page and audio removes the "I don't have time" objection.
3. **Comfort** — beautiful typography, dark/sepia modes, offline: the experience must look better than squinting at a phone.
4. **Personal discovery** — "books picked for you" answers "what should I read next?"
5. **Habit & goal** — "{read} of {goal} books this year" gives a reason to open the app daily.
6. **Narration quality** — human narrators or high-quality voices, only as true.
7. **Summaries** (if offered) — a fast path to big ideas for busy learners.

## Supporting elements

- **Cover stack/fan**: 3–5 real, licensed covers in one genre so the shot has a mood.
- **Player chips**: "1.5x", "Sleep timer 30 min", "Chapter {n} · {minutesLeft} min left".
- **Goal ring**: "{read}/{goal} books this year".
- **Highlight card**: a quoted line from a public-domain or in-house title with a highlight swipe.
- **Genre chips**: Thriller · Romance · Self-growth · Fantasy · History.
- **Notification**: "New in Fantasy: {title}" or "Your download is ready for the flight".
- **Stat**: "{hours} hours listened" from the user's own data.

## Visual direction

- Warm and literary: cream/paper backgrounds, a serif display face (Playfair Display, Lora, Canela-style) with a quiet sans for UI captions.
- Covers are the hero — let them break the frame (trio-fan); keep the device calm and centred.
- Night listening sets work in midnight/luxe with gold accents; daytime reading in cream/editorial.
- Genre-led apps (romance, web novels) go dramatic: rose and sunset gradients, character-driven covers — within all-ages limits.
- Leaders (Audible, Storytel, Kindle, Libby, Blinkist) show one hero cover per shot, generous whitespace and minimal UI chrome.
- Show real reading typography at a size that's legible in the thumbnail.

## Avoid

- Covers, titles or narrator names you don't have rights to show — publishers and narrators enforce this; Apple 5.2.1 and Google Play IP policy.
- "Unlimited", "every bestseller" or "free books" when the catalogue is limited or paywalled — Apple 2.3.1 / 3.1.2 subscription clarity; no prices (2.3.7).
- Summary apps implying the author's endorsement or "the full book in 15 minutes".
- Mature cover art in genre fiction — screenshots must suit all audiences even when the app is rated higher (Apple 2.3.8).
- Naming Kindle, Audible or Goodreads in captions (2.3.7, trademarks); importing from them is a feature line at most.
- Conversion mistakes: an empty shelf, a grid of 40 tiny covers, a dark reader that turns to mud at thumbnail size, a login screen as shot 1.
