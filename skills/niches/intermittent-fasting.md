# Intermittent Fasting

## The searcher

- Someone who heard about 16:8 from a friend or social media and wants a simple timer to start tonight.
- A weight-loss seeker who dislikes counting calories and is drawn to fasting as "one simple rule".
- An experienced faster who wants stages, history and longer protocols.
- What they want to feel: "A simple rule I can stick to, and a timer that keeps me honest."

## What wins the install

1. **The timer is the product.** A big progress ring with time remaining is understood in a split second, so make it shot 1.
2. **Simpler than counting calories.** "No counting, just timing" is the positioning that converts in this niche.
3. **Plan choice with guidance.** 16:8, 18:6, 20:4 and OMAD as cards with difficulty levels let beginners self-select safely.
4. **Educational stages.** A timeline of what may happen during a fast creates curiosity. Wording must stay careful and sourced.
5. **Reminders and streaks.** Adherence is the real job, and notifications prove the app helps.
6. **Progress.** Fasting history and a weight trend show it's working.

## Supporting elements

- Big progress ring with start time, goal end time and "{hours}h fasted".
- Stage timeline chips with approximate hours and cautious labels ("Blood sugar settles", "Fat burning may increase"), sourced in-app.
- Plan cards: 16:8 / 18:6 / 20:4 / OMAD with a difficulty tag and a "Recommended for beginners" chip.
- Notifications: "Your eating window opens in {minutes} min", "{hours}h fast complete".
- Streak calendar with completed fasts filled. A weight trend mini chart with the goal line.
- Water reminder chip: "{glasses} glasses today".

## Visual direction

- Sunset gradients (warm orange to pink) evoke the evening eating window. Mint for a fresh, health-first feel, midnight for a night-fasting or premium look.
- The ring timer is the hero, large and centered, with bold rounded numerals. Keep the device minimal around it.
- Use food imagery only around the eating window, and keep it wholesome and colorful.
- Leaders use warm gradients, very clean rings, stage timelines and minimalist cards. Science-heavy charts in early shots overwhelm.

## Avoid

- Health claims about autophagy, detox, anti-aging, "reverses diabetes" or "boosts immunity". These need strong evidence, fall under Apple 1.4.1 and Google Play's health misinformation policy, and are reviewed closely.
- Promoting extended or water fasts for weight loss without medical caveats. The app should flag that fasting isn't suitable during pregnancy, for minors, or with an eating-disorder history.
- Stage markers stated as precise physiological facts ("fat burning starts at exactly 12h"). Present them as approximate and educational.
- Before/after bodies, tape measures and rapid-loss promises (Apple 2.3.1).
- In Turkish, "oruç" carries religious meaning. Keep the framing secular wellness unless the app has a dedicated religious-fasting feature, and never imply religious authority.
- Prices, trials, "#1 fasting app" or fabricated "{users} fasters" counts.
- Conversion killers: a tiny timer, dense science charts in shots 1–3, and a missing plan-picker shot (beginners need to see where to start).
