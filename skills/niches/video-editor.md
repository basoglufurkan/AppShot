# Video Editor

## The searcher

- A short-form creator editing every day who wants pro-looking edits fast: captions, speed ramps, beat cuts.
- A vlogger or traveler stitching a pile of clips into one video with music.
- A small business owner making promo videos without hiring an editor.
- A casual user who just needs to trim a clip and add a song.
- What they want to feel: "That looks like a pro edit, and I did it on my phone in minutes."

## What wins the install

1. **The timeline as proof.** A multi-track timeline with clips, audio and keyframes instantly signals "real editor" to creators.
2. **Auto captions.** Word-by-word animated subtitles are the single biggest time-saver creators look for now.
3. **Trendy effects that are easy.** Speed ramps, transitions and beat sync are what people see in feeds and want to copy.
4. **Every platform format from one edit.** Aspect ratios and high-quality export remove a doubt.
5. **Templates for instant results.** Casual users want a finished edit, not a timeline.
6. **Clean export.** Watermark-free and 4K/60, but only if true for the tier shown. It's the top complaint about free editors.

## Supporting elements

- Timeline strip cutout: clip thumbnails, audio waveform, keyframe diamonds and a playhead.
- Caption style chips with a sample subtitle highlighting one word in accent color.
- Speed curve graph card (Montage, Hero, Bullet) with the curve drawn.
- Aspect ratio selector: 9:16 / 1:1 / 4:5 / 16:9.
- Export sheet card: "{resolution} · {fps} fps", using real options.
- Transition picker thumbnails and a layer stack: Text, Sticker, Overlay, Music.

## Visual direction

- Dark UI themes (midnight, neon) match editing apps and make footage glow. Use cyan or magenta accents. Mono suits a premium, minimalist editor.
- Use vivid, licensed footage (travel, sport, food, dance), shot vertically. Footage is the emotional hook, and the UI is the proof.
- Pull the timeline out of the device and enlarge it. It's unreadable at native screenshot scale.
- Typography: bold condensed sans for captions, plus a demo of creator-style caption fonts inside the frame.
- Category leaders use bright frames, word-highlight captions, floating panels and template grids. Their weakness is clutter, so one idea per shot wins.

## Avoid

- TikTok, Instagram or YouTube names and logos in captions implying partnership (Apple 5.2.1, Google Play impersonation). Say "short-form", "stories" or "shorts-ready".
- Copyrighted music or footage in demo frames (Apple 2.3.9). Use licensed stock or your own shoots.
- "No watermark", "4K" or "free" claims unless they hold for what the screenshot shows. No prices or trial banners.
- Android UI on iOS screenshots (Apple 2.3.10) and vice versa.
- Effects or results that need desktop software to recreate.
- No "#1 video editor", fake creator endorsements or invented export counts.
- Conversion killers: a full-device timeline nobody can read, menus with no footage, and five features crammed into one shot.
