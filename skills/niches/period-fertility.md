# Period & Fertility Tracker

## The searcher

- Someone who wants to know when their next period is coming, with no more surprises at work or on holiday. They search "period tracker".
- Someone trying to conceive who wants to understand their fertile window, often after months of uncertainty.
- Someone tracking PMS, cramps, mood or skin to understand their body's rhythm.
- A perimenopausal user (40s–50s) with irregular cycles and new symptoms. It's a growing and underserved segment.
- What they want to feel: "I understand my body, nothing catches me off guard, and my data is mine."

## What wins the install

1. **Know what's coming.** A cycle ring or calendar with the predicted next period is the core promise, understood in one glance.
2. **Privacy and data control.** Anonymous mode, local storage and easy deletion are decisive for many users. Show it, don't bury it.
3. **Body literacy.** Phase-based insights (energy, mood, skin) turn tracking into self-understanding.
4. **Fast symptom logging.** One-tap chips make daily logging feel effortless.
5. **Goal modes.** Track, conceive, pregnancy and perimenopause modes show the app grows with the user's life.
6. **Gentle, discreet reminders.** Wording that's private on a lock screen matters.

## Supporting elements

- Cycle ring: "Day {cycleDay}", phase label, "Period in {days} days", with predicted days dotted and logged days filled.
- Calendar with period, fertile window estimate and ovulation estimate in three soft tones, plus a legend.
- Symptom chips: Cramps, Headache, Bloating, Acne, Mood swings, Tender breasts.
- Phase insight card using "may" language: "Energy may dip in your luteal phase".
- Discreet notification: "Your cycle may start in 2 days" (lock-screen friendly).
- Privacy card: "Anonymous mode", "Stored on your device", "Delete anytime", only if true.
- Mode selector pills: Track / Conceive / Pregnancy / Perimenopause.

## Visual direction

- Rose, pastel, cream and calm palettes. Go beyond cliché all-pink with coral, plum, terracotta and warm neutrals, which feel more modern and inclusive.
- Soft illustrations of diverse women and abstract body or wave shapes. No explicit anatomy, blood or medical imagery.
- Typography: rounded friendly sans and warm, conversational captions.
- The circular cycle visual works as a motif across the set. Leaders use cycle rings, illustrated insight cards and calendar color-coding with plenty of breathing room.

## Avoid

- Contraception claims ("use as birth control", "safe days", "natural contraception") unless the app has regulatory clearance as a contraceptive (FDA, CE). That's a medical-device claim under Apple 1.4.1 and Google Play's health policy.
- Guarantees about conception or prediction certainty. Always "estimate" or "predicted".
- Diagnostic claims (PCOS or endometriosis detection) without clinical validation.
- Privacy messaging that doesn't match the privacy policy and labels. Health data shared with advertisers is an Apple 5.1.3 issue, and period apps have a public enforcement history (FTC).
- Explicit or bloody imagery (Apple 1.1.4). Keep visuals abstract and dignified.
- For teen users, age-appropriate copy and no sexualized framing.
- Prices, trials, "#1 period tracker" or fabricated "{users} women trust us" claims.
- Conversion killers: pink-on-pink clichés that alienate many users, dense medical charts, and no privacy shot at all.
