# AI Video Generator

## The searcher

- A short-form creator who needs scroll-stopping clips without filming. They search "ai video generator" or "text to video".
- Someone who just saw a viral trend (an old photo coming alive, a pet dancing, an "AI hug") and wants to make one with their own photo. They're template-driven and impatient.
- A faceless-channel owner or small business making ads and shorts on a budget.
- What they want to feel: "I typed one sentence and got something that looks like a movie."

## What wins the install

1. **Motion in a static medium.** A screenshot can't play, so imply movement with frame strips, motion streaks, play states and a panorama across shots. Let the App Preview video carry the real proof.
2. **Prompt next to result.** Showing the exact prompt beside the output frame makes the result feel attainable, not like a showreel.
3. **Trend templates.** Most consumer installs come from one specific trend. A grid of real, current templates converts that intent immediately.
4. **"Your photo comes alive."** Personal photos (pets, family, selfies) beat abstract sci-fi landscapes for mass-market installs.
5. **Creative control for prosumers.** Camera motion, style and aspect ratio chips signal depth to creators.
6. **Platform-ready output.** Vertical 9:16 for shorts removes a "will this work for me" doubt.

## Supporting elements

- Prompt card: "{realPrompt}" typed with a blinking cursor and an arrow into the output frame.
- Film strip: 3–4 genuine consecutive frames showing the motion progression.
- Play button overlay plus a duration chip: "{duration}s · {resolution}".
- Style chips: Cinematic, Anime, 3D, Realistic. Camera chips: Zoom in, Orbit, Pan.
- Aspect toggles: 9:16 / 16:9 / 1:1.
- Generation progress ring: "Rendering {percent}%", showing the honest state.
- Trend pills with the app's real template names.

## Visual direction

- Go dark and cinematic (neon, midnight): letterbox bars, light film grain, glowing edges. The output frame should be huge and the device secondary.
- Run the bridge layout across shots 2–3 so one panoramic frame sequence flows over two screenshots. It's the cheapest way to say "motion".
- Add subtle motion streaks or ghosting around the frame edges only. The generated frame itself stays untouched.
- Typography: bold condensed sans or a cinematic serif, caption at the top, highlight in a hot accent (magenta, electric blue).
- Consumer leaders lead with faces, pets and trend grids, with a glowing Generate button. Prosumer tools lead with epic landscape frames and camera controls. Pick one audience for shot 1.
- Ship an App Preview / promo video. In this niche it often outperforms the screenshots themselves.

## Avoid

- Deepfakes of real people, celebrities or politicians in demo content. No non-consensual or sexualized templates, and no "kiss anyone" marketing. This trips Google Play's AI-generated content and sexual content policies and Apple 1.1 and 5.2.
- Copyrighted characters, film stills, logos or music in sample outputs (Apple 2.3.9).
- Model names (Sora, Veo, Kling…) styled as if the app were theirs. "Powered by" only where the provider's terms allow it, and never their logo as your hero.
- Outputs better than typical generation, composited or color-graded afterwards. Cherry-picking real outputs is fine. Doctoring them isn't.
- Speed promises ("in seconds") the queue can't keep. No credits, coin packs, prices or "free" in screenshots.
- Grief marketing ("talk to your late mother again"). It's sensitive, draws backlash and invites reviewer attention.
- Undisclosed AI content. Play also requires in-app reporting of offensive generations.
- Conversion killers: a tiny output inside a full device frame, no sense of motion anywhere in the set, and every shot in the same style.
