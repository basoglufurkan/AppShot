# Yoga & Pilates

## The searcher

- Someone who wants gentle, feel-good movement at home, often after a stressful stretch or a long sitting day. They search "yoga for beginners".
- A trend-driven searcher (often a woman aged 25–55) who saw "wall pilates" or "somatic exercises" on social media and wants a guided program to tone and stand taller.
- A desk worker with a stiff neck and back who wants short mobility sessions.
- What they want to feel: "I feel better in my body, at my own pace, without being judged."

## What wins the install

1. **Feeling over sweat.** Calm, aspirational yet attainable imagery tells the viewer this is kind, not punishing. The mood of the set is the promise.
2. **A plan for my level and goal.** A quiz result (flexibility, posture, toning, stress) makes the program feel personal and is the main conversion driver.
3. **Short sessions.** 10–20 minute classes fit real life and kill "I don't have time".
4. **Expert guidance.** Clear instruction, modifications and pose names reassure beginners and protect bodies.
5. **The trend program.** Wall pilates, chair yoga and somatic challenges capture high-volume trend searches. Name the one the app actually has.
6. **Gentle progress.** Minutes, streaks and flexibility milestones without aggressive gamification.

## Supporting elements

- Pose card: pose name in English (plus Sanskrit where the app shows it) and a "Hold {seconds}s" ring.
- Session card: "{minutes} min · Beginner · No equipment".
- Quiz result card: Goal Flexibility / Posture / Core, with a plan length chip.
- Soft weekly minutes ring and a dotted streak (dots, not flames).
- Breath cue bubble: "Inhale… 4".
- Category pills: Wall Pilates, Morning Flow, Mobility, Yin, Chair, Somatic.
- Reminder notification: "Your 10-min morning flow is ready".

## Visual direction

- Calm, cream, rose and pastel palettes: soft natural light, plants, linen, wood floors, generous whitespace.
- Typography: elegant light serif headlines with a clean sans for small labels. Lowercase or sentence-case captions feel kinder than all-caps.
- Full-bleed lifestyle photos lead and the device sits smaller and overlaid. Use real instructors of varied ages and bodies, in neutral activewear.
- Leaders in this niche use beige/sand palettes, wall pilates challenge calendars, and serene morning-light studio shots. Neon fitness palettes signal the wrong product.

## Avoid

- Therapeutic claims ("cures back pain", "fixes scoliosis", "detox your organs", "heals trauma"). Apple 1.4.1 and Google Play's health misinformation policy apply. Say "feel looser", "move with ease".
- Pregnancy, injury or post-surgery programs without in-app guidance to check with a professional.
- Weight-loss transformation imagery and "lose 10 kg with wall pilates" promises (Apple 2.3.1, Play deceptive behavior).
- Extreme advanced poses as the hook. They intimidate the beginners who make up most of the search volume.
- Religious symbols used as decoration, which is a brand risk in several markets. Instructor photos without model releases (Apple 2.3.9).
- Prices, "free trial" or discount text in screenshots. No invented "{users} women joined" counts.
- Conversion killers: dark, harsh high-energy themes, text-heavy feature lists in shot 1, and studio stock that looks like a gym ad.
