# To-Do & Tasks

## The searcher

- Overwhelmed: too many things in their head, sticky notes everywhere, a vague dread of forgetting something important.
- Switching from paper, the default reminders app or a notes app that became a graveyard of lists.
- A small team or couple who need to share and assign tasks without a heavy project tool.
- People who struggle with focus and want gentle structure and small wins (keep it non-medical).
- After installing they want to feel: a clear head, one calm list for today, and the little hit of checking something off.

## What wins the install

1. **Calm and clarity** — from chaos to one tidy Today list; sell the feeling, not the feature count.
2. **Capture in seconds** — natural-language quick add ("Pay rent every 1st at 9am") parsed into date, repeat and project.
3. **Reminders that land at the right moment** — time- and location-based, on every device.
4. **Focus on today** — priorities and a short Today view beat endless backlogs.
5. **Everywhere** — widgets, lock screen, watch, Siri/Assistant: capture without opening the app.
6. **Share the load** — shared lists, assignees, comments.

## Supporting elements

- **Quick-add bubble**: "Call mom tomorrow 6pm #family" with parsed chips highlighted.
- **Notifications**: "Submit report — due in 1 hour", "Buy flowers — you're near the market".
- **Completion stat**: "{completed} tasks done this week" / streak chip.
- **Priority flags** and a single satisfying check-mark animation frame.
- **Widget mock**: Today list on the Home Screen; watch complication.
- **Assignee avatars** with a short comment bubble.
- **Before/after**: sticky-note chaos vs a clean list.

## Visual direction

- Minimal and airy: lots of whitespace, one accent colour (red, blue or volt), mono or soft-gray backgrounds; dark mode for a premium "Things-like" calm.
- System-native typography (SF Pro, Inter); the UI should look effortless, not powerful-and-complicated.
- Keep lists short (5–7 items) and realistic; a 20-row list communicates stress.
- Leaders (Todoist, Things, TickTick, Microsoft To Do) win with restraint — one idea per shot, big checkboxes, soft shadows.
- Zoom into parsed text and check marks; they're the product's micro-delights.
- Light sets for broad audiences; dark sets for pro/power-user positioning.

## Avoid

- Medical claims: "treats ADHD", "clinically proven focus" — health claims need evidence (Apple 1.4.1, Google Play health policies); "designed with ADHD users in mind" only if genuinely true.
- Productivity statistics ("2× more productive", "save 10 hours a week") without data (Apple 2.3.1).
- Siri, Apple Watch or Google Assistant marks used outside platform guidelines; on iOS never show Android devices and vice versa (Apple 2.3.10).
- Competitor names in captions ("Todoist alternative") (2.3.7).
- Lorem ipsum or placeholder task names — reads as unfinished and can trigger a rejection.
- Conversion mistakes: a feature grid before the feeling, 12-pt list text, an empty state as the hook, too many accent colours.
