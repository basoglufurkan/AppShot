# Running & Cycling

## The searcher

- A new runner who wants to go from zero to their first 5K and needs a plan that won't hurt them. They search "couch to 5k" or "running for beginners".
- Someone training for a race (10K, half marathon) who wants a day-by-day plan ending on race day.
- A cyclist who wants ride stats, routes and elevation without a bike computer.
- What they want to feel: "I know my pace, I see my route, and I'm getting to the finish line."

## What wins the install

1. **Map and stats beauty.** A bold route line on a map with pace and distance is the iconic image of this niche, and it instantly communicates "GPS tracker".
2. **A finish-line goal.** C25K and race plans sell an outcome ("run your first 5K"), not a tracker.
3. **Audio coaching.** Run without looking at the phone. Voice cues answer "how do I use this while running?"
4. **Progress and PRs.** Fastest 5K, weekly distance and streaks keep people coming back.
5. **Integrations.** Watch support, Health sync and compatibility with popular platforms matter to existing runners.
6. **Safety.** Live location sharing is a strong differentiator, especially for solo and night runners.

## Supporting elements

- Stat trio card: Distance {distance} · Pace {pace} · Time {time}.
- Route polyline in the accent color on a muted dark map, with start and finish pins.
- Audio cue notification: "{distance} km done · Avg pace {pace}".
- Elevation profile strip under the map.
- PR badge: "Fastest 5K · {time}". Heart rate zone bar (Z1–Z5).
- Plan calendar with a "Race day" flag on the final square.
- Live tracking card: "Sharing your run with {contact}".

## Visual direction

- Outdoors at sunrise or sunset, with motion blur and real streets and trails. Volt and ember for performance, ocean for calm endurance, midnight for night-run and safety angles.
- Maps: high-contrast route line on a desaturated dark basemap, with the attribution kept visible.
- Typography: big condensed numerals with tabular figures for pace and distance, and short punchy captions.
- Runners and cyclists of different ages, bodies and genders, with unbranded gear.
- Leaders use a full-bleed athlete photo with phone and watch, maps as hero, and big stat numbers. Indoor treadmill stock undercuts the outdoor GPS promise.

## Avoid

- Strava, Garmin or Apple Watch logos and names beyond their official badges and brand guidelines (Apple 5.2.1). No "Strava alternative" in captions.
- Map screenshots with the provider attribution removed. Mapbox, OSM and Apple Maps terms require it to stay visible.
- Demo routes that reveal a real home address or start point. Use public parks and city routes.
- "Most accurate GPS" or exact calorie claims without proof.
- Medical heart claims ("detects arrhythmia", "prevents heart disease"). That's medical-device territory under Apple 1.4.1 and Google Play's health policy.
- Fake PRs, fabricated runner counts or "#1 running app".
- Prices or trial text in screenshots.
- Conversion killers: indoor stock photos, stats too small to read, generic "track your runs" hooks with no goal, and every shot being the same map.
