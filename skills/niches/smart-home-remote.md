# Smart Home & TV Remote

## The searcher

- Two jobs share this niche — TV remote / screen mirroring, and smart-home control — keep the set on the one the app does best.
- Lost the remote down the sofa, or the batteries died — urgent, slightly annoyed, wants it working in 30 seconds.
- Tired of typing passwords and searches with arrow keys; wants a phone keyboard and touchpad for the TV.
- Wants to throw photos, videos or the phone screen onto the TV for family and friends.
- The smart-home owner with five brand apps who wants lights, plugs, thermostat and cameras in one place. After installing everyone wants to feel: instant control — it just works.

## What wins the install

1. **"Will it work with my TV?" answered** — the compatibility objection decides installs; show supported brands/platforms as plain text.
2. **Connects in seconds** — auto-discovery on the same Wi-Fi, no pairing headaches.
3. **Better than the plastic remote** — touchpad swipes, keyboard, voice search, quick app launch.
4. **Screen mirroring/casting** — photos and videos on the big screen.
5. **One app for the whole home** — rooms, scenes and automations across brands (Matter where supported).
6. **Always at hand** — widgets, Lock Screen and Control Center-style quick access.

## Story (shot by shot)

1. **Hook** — your phone is the remote — show: sleek remote UI with D-pad and touchpad — layout: hero-device — angle: phone in front of a softly glowing TV in a cosy room.
2. **Works with yours** — compatibility at a glance — show: device discovery / brand list — layout: feature-list — angle: supported platforms as text (only real ones) + "Connected" state.
3. **Touch & type** — faster than arrow keys — show: touchpad and keyboard input — layout: zoom-callout — angle: zoom on typing a search directly on the TV.
4. **Mirror** — your photos on the big screen — show: casting/mirroring flow — layout: duo-devices — angle: phone gallery → TV showing the photo.

6. **Automate** — scenes in one tap — show: "Movie night" scene + automation — layout: ui-pieces — angle: scene card, schedule chip, widget.

## Supporting elements

- **Remote UI pieces**: D-pad, touchpad with a swipe trail, volume rocker, app-launch shortcuts.
- **Connection pill**: "Connected to Living Room TV".
- **Keyboard bubble** typing a search query that appears on the TV.
- **Device tiles**: "Living room light · On", "Thermostat {temp}°", "Plug · Off", "Front door camera".
- **Scene card**: "Movie night — lights dim, TV on, AC {temp}°".
- **Notifications**: "Motion detected at front door" (demo footage only), "AC turned on — arrive to {temp}°".
- **Compatibility row**: platform names as text — only those genuinely supported.

## Visual direction

- Premium and tactile: dark glossy UI (midnight, glass) or minimal mono, with one accent glow (cyan, volt) for active states.
- Ambient living-room photography (licensed) with a TV glow behind the device; warm light signals "home".
- Remote apps often look cheap — clean spacing, real-looking controls and consistent icons are a differentiator.
- Leaders (Google Home, Apple Home, SmartThings, Roku's own app) show rooms and one action per shot; universal-remote apps lead with compatibility.
- Keep TV screens showing neutral, self-made content (no streaming brands, no copyrighted shows).
- Device tiles large and few (4–6) so states read in the thumbnail.

## Avoid

- Implying official status: "Samsung Remote", brand logos, or UI imitating a manufacturer's own remote — impersonation and trademark issues (Apple 4.1, 5.2.1; Google Play impersonation policy). Use "for {brand} TVs" only for genuinely supported devices.
- Claiming IR control on iPhone — iPhones have no IR blaster; "works with any TV" is false (Apple 2.3.1). AC remotes on iOS need Wi-Fi-connected units.
- "Works with Apple Home", Matter or Alexa badges without the actual certification or programme approval.
- Real security-camera footage of people; streaming-service logos or copyrighted shows on the TV screen.
- Android devices in iOS screenshots or vice versa (2.3.10); prices or "free" when features are premium (2.3.7).
- Conversion mistakes: a skeuomorphic plastic remote, an endless brand list, a pairing/permission screen as shot 1, no TV or home context.
