# Islamic Prayer & Quran

## The searcher

- A practising Muslim who wants **correct times for where they are** and an adhan alert they can trust. They search at
  the moment of need ("prayer times", "adhan time", "fajr time") and often use loose spellings ("azan", "salat",
  "quran").
- A traveller, student or expat in an unfamiliar city who needs local times and the qibla fast, often offline. A
  listing in a community's language also reaches its diaspora abroad, because the App Store shows the device's language.
- Someone building a Quran habit: reading daily, finishing a khatm in Ramadan, listening on the commute, and
  understanding through a trusted translation.
- A seasonal wave around Ramadan (suhoor and iftar countdowns, taraweeh), Laylat al-Qadr, Jumu'ah and the Eid days.
  Search interest climbs in the weeks before Ramadan, so seasonal pages need lead time.
- A wide age range. Older users want large, simple screens. Younger users want widgets, the lock screen and Apple
  Watch. Both want calm: an ad popping up during worship is a frequent complaint in this niche's reviews.
- After installing they want to feel sure of the time, calm, and not distracted.
  Scope: prayer times (salah, adhan), qibla, Quran (translation, tafsir, khatm), dua, dhikr (tasbih) and
  Ramadan (suhoor and iftar timetable). Christian apps use `bible-prayer.md`.

## What wins the install

1. **Accuracy you can trust.** Times for my location using the method I follow (MWL, ISNA, Umm al-Qura, a national
   religious authority's published times and others) and an adhan that arrives on time. For most users this is the
   whole product.
2. **A qibla that works.** A clear compass with a calibration hint, plus a map or AR view if the app has one.
3. **A reverent Quran reader.** Clean Arabic script, a translation alongside, transliteration, recitation audio, and
   juz/surah navigation that resumes where you left off.
4. **Ramadan tools.** Suhoor and iftar timetable, iftar countdown, a suhoor alarm, and khatm tracking for the month.
5. **Calm and respect.** Ad-light or ad-free (only if true), sober design, nothing gamified about worship.
6. **Glanceability.** Next-prayer countdown in widgets, on the lock screen and on the Watch.
7. **Privacy.** Location used on the device, no account needed (only if true). Religious data is sensitive, and users
   notice.

## Supporting elements

- **Prayer strip** (in the captured UI): Fajr · Sunrise · Dhuhr · Asr · Maghrib · Isha with {times} computed **by the
  app** for the stated sample city and date. Record the city, date and method in `brief.md`.
- **Countdown card**: "Next: Maghrib in {minutes} min".
- **Notifications**: "Dhuhr in 15 minutes" · "Maghrib adhan · {city}" · "Iftar in {minutes} minutes"
  · "Wake you for suhoor at {time}?". Keep them to features the app really has, and use no emoji.
- **Qibla card**: "{bearing}° · {distance} km to the Kaaba" from the app's real calculation.
- **Quran card**: a complete ayah in Arabic with its translation and reference (for example "Al-Baqarah 2:255"), in
  the translation the app is licensed to show.
- **Khatm progress**: "Juz {juz} · {percent}%" · "Last read: {surah}, {ayah}".
- **Dhikr counter**: "{count}/33 · SubhanAllah", plus the post-prayer tasbih sequence.
- **Ramadan row**: "Ramadan day {day} · Suhoor ends {time} · Iftar {time}" (seasonal page only).
- **Method chips**: "MWL", "ISNA", "Hanafi Asr" (only the methods the app supports).

## Visual direction

- **Themes:** `forest` (deep green and cream serif, the classic look), `luxe` (black and gold, ideal for Ramadan and
  Quran sets), `calm` (night indigo with stars for fajr, taraweeh and Laylat al-Qadr), `cream` (parchment for Quran
  reading), `midnight` (modern premium), `soft-gray` (utility prayer-time apps that should feel native). Use one
  accent, gold or green.
- **Typography:** a serif display face (Fraunces, Playfair, DM Serif) for Quran and Ramadan, a calm grotesk for
  prayer-time utilities. Keep Arabic **inside the captured UI**, where the app renders
  it correctly. Don't type Arabic into English captions: it falls back to a system face at tight
  leading and can clip harakat.
- **Decor:** restrained. `glow` behind the device, `rings` behind the qibla compass, the faint `grid` or `dots`
  for a geometric hint, `sparkles` (as stars) only on `calm`. Never `confetti`, `shapes` or `beams`.
- **Imagery:** no figurative depictions of prophets or companions. No stock photos of people praying. Kaaba and
  mosque photos only with rights. Calm night skies and architecture details beat kitsch.
- **Sacred text:** complete ayahs with correct harakat and reference. Never let a `zoom.rect` or `ui-pieces` crop cut
  through an ayah. Never put `**highlight**` on Allah's names or ayah text: highlight the benefit word
  ("**prayer**", "**qibla**").
- **Device:** upright (`hero-device`, `device-top`). Avoid tilts, because they feel playful here. For Play use
  `storeOverrides.android.theme.device.frame = "flat"`.
- **Light and dark:** many users run the app in dark mode for night prayers. Match the capture to the theme and check
  both modes in `review/search-<locale>.png`.

## Avoid

- **Religious authority claims:** "approved by {authority}", "official app", "the most accurate times", "authentic",
  rulings or fatwas in captions. Mention a religious authority only as the real data source, only if the app actually
  uses its published times, and never so that it implies endorsement (Apple 5.2.1, Play impersonation policy).
- **Spiritual promises:** "your dua will be accepted", "earn rewards", "whoever reads this dua…", "your wish comes
  true", healing, wealth or protection claims. These are misleading under Apple 2.3.1 and 1.4.1 and Play's
  deceptive-behaviour policy, and offensive to many users.
- **Inaccuracy:** times that don't match the app's calculation for the shown city and date, a qibla without a real
  computation, or a method the app doesn't offer. Reviews in this niche punish one wrong prayer time hard.
- **Sectarian or regional bias:** mocking or excluding other traditions. Show only the madhab and method options the
  app really supports (Hanafi/Shafi'i Asr, Ja'fari). Keep the language inclusive.
- **Gamifying worship:** 🔥 streaks, trophies, "level up your iman", confetti, leaderboards of who prayed most. Use
  quiet progress (checkmarks, "consistent") instead, and no emoji in captions or notifications.
- **Privacy:** religious belief is special-category data (GDPR Art. 9).
  Use fictional demo data only: no real names in dua requests, no real home location, no prayer logs of a real
  person. Claims like "Your location stays on your device" or "No account needed" only if true and consistent with
  the App Privacy label and Play Data safety form.
- **Copyright:** many Quran translations, recitation recordings and mushaf fonts are licensed. Show only what the app
  has rights to, even in screenshots.
- **Money:** zakat, sadaqah or donation screens (Apple 3.2.1(vi) limits in-app donations to approved nonprofits),
  prices, and "free" on Play. If ads are removed only in Pro, tag the shot "Pro" (Apple 2.3.2).
- **Seasonal copy in the evergreen set:** Ramadan, Laylat al-Qadr or Eid lines belong on a custom product page or a
  timed update (Ramadan 2027 is around early February to early March, so confirm the official calendar). Swap them
  back right after.
- **Captured ads:** never capture an ad banner, interstitial or debug state.
- **Mixing traditions** or Christian vocabulary unless the app is interfaith. Christian apps belong in `bible-prayer.md`.
