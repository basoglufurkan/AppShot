# Habit Tracker

## The searcher

- Someone riding new-year, new-month or "this Monday" energy who wants to finally build a routine. They search "habit tracker" or "daily routine".
- A reader of a popular habits book who wants streaks, tracking and a "don't break the chain" system.
- A productivity enthusiast who loves stats, heatmaps and widgets.
- Someone trying to cut a bad habit gently rather than with willpower alone.
- What they want to feel: "A satisfying daily check-off, and proof I'm becoming consistent."

## What wins the install

1. **The satisfying check.** Completed rings, filled circles and streak flames feel good. The screenshot must show completion, not an empty list.
2. **Visual proof of consistency.** Heatmaps and calendars turn effort into something you can see, and that's the core motivation.
3. **Setup in seconds.** Picking an icon, color and frequency shows it's simple, not another complex productivity system.
4. **Smart reminders and widgets.** The daily nudge and home-screen glance are what make habits stick.
5. **Flexibility for real life.** Weekly targets, skip days and streak freezes kill the fear of failing on day 4.
6. **Personal aesthetic.** Colors, icons and themes make the tracker feel like theirs.

## Supporting elements

- Habit rows with check animation states and a flame chip "{streak}".
- Heatmap calendar card in contribution-graph style, in the habit's color.
- Completion stat: "{rate}% this month", plus a best-streak badge.
- Home-screen widget mockup with today's habits.
- Notification: "Read 10 pages: tap to complete", in the app's real copy.
- Icon and color picker cutout. Frequency chips: Daily, 3× a week, Weekdays.
- Streak freeze or skip-day chip, which reassures perfectionists.

## Visual direction

- Clean-light or mono minimalism for a productivity positioning. Playful or mint with bright habit colors for casual and Gen Z audiences.
- Colorful habit chips and satisfying filled states on calm backgrounds. Let color carry the joy.
- Typography: rounded geometric sans with big streak numerals.
- Leaders use colorful habit grids, heatmaps, widgets on the home screen and gentle mascots. Plain checklists look like a to-do app and lose the click.

## Avoid

- Book titles and authors' names (Atomic Habits) in captions. That's a trademark issue (Apple 2.3.7 / 5.2.1). Users can still find you through the keyword field.
- Health claims attached to habits ("cures anxiety", "lose 10 kg"). Habits are the product, not outcomes you can't guarantee.
- Implausible demo data (a 900-day streak on 30 habits) that looks fake and intimidates beginners.
- Prices, "lifetime deal" banners or trial text in screenshots. No "#1 habit app" or invented "{users} habits built".
- Conversion killers: an empty habit list as the hook, 20 habits on one screen, and dull checklists with no visual progress.
