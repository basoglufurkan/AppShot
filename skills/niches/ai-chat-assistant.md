# AI Chat Assistant

## The searcher

- Someone who keeps hearing "just ask AI" and wants a chat on their phone that works with no learning curve. They type "ai chat", "chatbot", "ask ai".
- A student at 11 p.m. stuck on homework or an essay outline. They want it explained, not just answered.
- A professional writing emails in a second language who needs the tone polished in seconds, between meetings.
- Someone who already uses a big-name assistant and wants something faster, voice-first, more private or better in their language. They need a visible reason to switch.
- What they want to feel after install: "I can ask anything and get a useful answer right now."

## What wins the install

1. **A real, useful answer in shot 1.** The product is the conversation, so one crisp question-and-answer pair proves quality faster than any adjective.
2. **Use-case breadth in shots 2–3.** Searchers often don't know what they'd use it for. Concrete jobs (email, homework, trip plan, a photo question) create the "oh, I need that" moment.
3. **A visible reason to switch from the default assistant.** Voice mode, photo/document input, no sign-up, a specific language or privacy. The category is crowded (Apple 4.3 spam risk), so sameness kills both review and conversion.
4. **Speed and simplicity.** Streaming text and one-tap prompt chips beat the "it's complicated / it's just another chatbot" objection.
5. **Trust.** Clear data controls and chat history answer "where do my chats go?", which now decides more installs than it used to.
6. **Proof, only when it's real.** A strong genuine rating helps late in the set. A weak one hurts, so leave it out.

## Story (shot by shot)

1. **Hook** — outcome promise — show: one conversation with a short, relatable prompt and a 3–5 line answer, zoomed so it reads at thumbnail size — layout: chat-bubbles — angle: ask anything, get a clear answer instantly.
2. **Breadth** — "it does that too" — show: home screen with prompt suggestions or category tiles — layout: screen-grid — angle: one app for writing, studying, planning and more.
3. **Differentiator** — the reason to switch — show: the input mode the default assistant does worse (voice waveform, camera scan, PDF upload) — layout: hero-device — angle: talk, snap or upload instead of typing.
4. **Writing** — time saved — show: an email or message rewrite with tone options — layout: ui-pieces — angle: the right words in seconds.
5. **Learning** — understanding, not copying — show: a step-by-step explanation or photo-of-a-problem answer — layout: zoom-callout — angle: get it explained your way.
6. **Personal** — make it yours — show: personas, tone or model settings — layout: floating-cards — angle: an assistant that writes like you.
7. **Trust** — kill the last objection — show: history, privacy controls, languages — layout: feature-list — angle: your chats stay under your control (only if true).

If the app is voice-first, swap 1 and 3: open with the voice screen and a live transcript bubble.

## Supporting elements

- Chat pair: a user bubble "{realPrompt}" and an assistant bubble with a streaming cursor. Use a real prompt the app actually answers well.
- Prompt chip row: "Write an email", "Explain this", "Plan my week", "Fix my grammar". Pull these from the app's real suggestions.
- Input mode pills floating near the composer: mic, camera, file.
- Tone switcher card: Formal / Friendly / Short, each with the same sentence rewritten (a text comparison row).
- Language stack card: flags or language names with "{languages} languages".
- Speed chip: "Answers in ~{seconds}s", only if measured on a normal connection.
- Privacy badge: "No account needed" or "History on device", only if true.
- Model badge: "Powered by {model}", only where the provider's brand terms allow it.

## Visual direction

- Dark themes (midnight, glass) read "AI / pro" and make chat bubbles pop. Use aurora gradients for a consumer, playful feel and clean-light for business positioning.
- Use a modern geometric sans with heavy captions. Text inside the device has to read at search-result size, so crop or enlarge the conversation rather than showing a whole tiny screen.
- Put the hero device straight-on and let bubbles break out of the frame and scale up 120–140%.
- Use one electric accent (violet, teal or acid green) on the highlight word and the send button, nowhere else.
- Top apps show a huge conversation, "Ask anything" hooks, use-case grids and voice waveforms. Robot mascots, glowing brains and circuit-board stock art look dated and cheap.

## Avoid

- Model brand names ("ChatGPT", "GPT", "Gemini", "Claude") or their logos used in a way that implies an official app. This trips Apple 4.1 (copycats) and 5.2.1 (IP) and Google Play's impersonation policy, and most providers' brand rules forbid it.
- Logo-only or empty-chat screenshots. Apple 2.3.3 wants the app in use, and an empty composer converts nothing.
- Answers the app can't reliably reproduce, or that contain factual errors. Reviewers and users try the same prompt.
- Medical, legal or financial advice as hero examples. They invite Apple 1.4 scrutiny and imply professional advice.
- Hinting at unlimited free usage when there are message caps. No prices, plan names, "free" or trial copy in screenshots.
- "Private" claims the data flow doesn't support. Apple 5.1.2(i) now requires disclosure and consent before personal data goes to third-party AI.
- Marketing "uncensored", "no filter", jailbreak or NSFW chat. Google Play's AI-generated content policy also requires in-app reporting of offensive output.
- No "#1 AI app", invented ratings or user counts. On Play, no "Download now" style calls to action in the graphics.
- Conversion killers: tiny unreadable conversations, "smart AI" headlines with no job attached, and seven shots that all look like the same chat.
