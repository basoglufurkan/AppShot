# Study & Flashcards

## The searcher

- A student with a date circled: finals, MCAT/USMLE/NCLEX, bar exam, or in Turkey YKS, KPSS, ALES. Anxious, behind, looking for the most efficient hour possible.
- Drowning in material: lecture slides, PDFs, textbook chapters — and no time to hand-write hundreds of cards.
- Burned by rereading: knows highlighting "feels" productive but nothing sticks on exam day.
- Lifelong learners and hobbyists (trivia, medicine, coding, languages) who want a personal memory system.
- After installing they want to feel: "I know exactly what to study today — and I'll still remember it next month."

## What wins the install

1. **AI card generation from your own notes** — kills the biggest friction (making cards takes forever); show PDF/notes turning into a deck.
2. **The memory mechanism, in plain words** — "review right before you forget" is memorable; algorithm names (SM-2, FSRS) are not.
3. **Only what's due** — "{due} cards today, ~{minutes} min" turns a mountain into a task.
4. **Exam-specific readiness** — ready-made decks or tags for the user's exam, if the app has them.
5. **Mastery you can see** — percentage per deck answers "am I ready?"
6. **Rich cards** — images, audio, formulas, diagrams for medicine, chemistry, languages.
7. **Sync and offline** — study on the bus, in the library basement, on any device.

## Story (shot by shot)

1. **Hook** — your notes become a study deck — show: PDF/lecture notes on one side, generated cards on the other — layout: before-after — angle: messy input, clean output.
2. **Recall** — remember it on exam day — show: a card mid-flip with answer buttons — layout: hero-device — angle: one great card, big and readable.
3. **Today** — only what's due — show: home/review queue — layout: big-number — angle: "{due} cards due · {minutes} min".
4. **Smart scheduling** — review before you forget — show: next-review intervals or answer buttons (Again / Hard / Good / Easy) — layout: zoom-callout — angle: zoom on the intervals.
5. **Modes** — never boring — show: quiz, match, write, cloze — layout: trio-fan — angle: three modes side by side.
6. **Readiness** — know what you know — show: deck mastery/stats — layout: floating-cards — angle: mastery rings floating around the device.
7. **Everywhere** — rich cards, sync, offline — show: image/formula card + device list — layout: feature-list — angle: 4 short proof points.

## Supporting elements

- **Flip card**: front question, back answer, slight 3D tilt, floating outside the frame.
- **Due counter**: "{due} cards due today", "Next review in {days} days".
- **Answer buttons zoom**: Again / Hard / Good / Easy with their intervals.
- **Mastery ring**: "{percent}% mastered · {deckName}".
- **Import chips**: PDF · Slides · Photo · Text — only formats the app supports.
- **Forgetting-curve sketch**: an illustrative curve labelled as illustration, never as user data.
- **Comparison**: "Rereading notes" vs "Active recall".
- **Notification**: "{due} cards ready — a {minutes}-minute session".

## Visual direction

- Focus and calm: midnight with one electric accent for the late-night med-student vibe, or clean-light with a confident blue for school audiences.
- Crisp sans (Inter, SF Pro) with tabular numerals for counters; formulas rendered properly (LaTeX), never as pixelated images.
- Cards are the hero object — stacked, fanned or flipping out of the device; keep card text at least 3× body size.
- Colour-code difficulty buttons consistently across all shots.
- Leaders (Quizlet, Anki, Brainscape, RemNote) range from bright and friendly to utilitarian; differentiate with clarity and big type rather than more features.
- Use real-looking but self-written study content (your own biology, history, vocabulary cards).

## Avoid

- Score or pass guarantees ("Pass the MCAT", "+20 points") — Apple 2.3.1 and consumer law; use "prepare", "practise", "review".
- Implying official status: MCAT, USMLE, SAT, YKS, KPSS are trademarks/official exams; "for {exam} prep" is fine, "official" is not. Don't reproduce real ÖSYM or licensed exam questions — they are copyrighted.
- Naming Anki or Quizlet in captions ("Anki alternative") — Apple 2.3.7 forbids other app names in metadata.
- Copyrighted textbook pages, figures or a professor's slides in screenshots.
- AI-generated cards with visible errors — students zoom in.
- Prices or "free" when the AI generator is paywalled.
- Conversion mistakes: an empty deck list, tiny card text, algorithm jargon on shot 1, ten modes on one screen.
