# Fashion & Outfit Planner

## The searcher

- Someone with a full closet who still has "nothing to wear" every morning and wants outfit ideas from what they already own.
- A style-curious user who wants an AI or personal stylist to suggest looks for the weather, an occasion or their body shape.
- A minimalist building a capsule wardrobe, or someone trying to shop less and wear more.
- A traveler planning outfits and a packing list for a trip.
- What they want to feel: "I look put-together without overthinking, and I actually use what I own."

## What wins the install

1. **Outfits from your own closet.** Shot 1 should show a styled outfit made from the user's items. It answers "nothing to wear" directly.
2. **An effortless digital closet.** Snap a piece and the background disappears. Setup looks fast, which kills the biggest objection ("digitizing my closet takes forever").
3. **AI stylist suggestions.** Weather- and occasion-aware outfit ideas feel like having a stylist.
4. **Planning.** An outfit calendar and packing lists turn inspiration into routine.
5. **Insights and the sustainability angle.** Cost per wear, most and least worn. "Shop less, wear more" resonates strongly now.
6. **Virtual try-on (if the app has it).** Highly compelling, but only with honest results.

## Story (shot by shot)

1. **Hook** — the outcome — show: a beautiful flat-lay outfit card titled "Today's outfit" with a weather chip — layout: full-bleed — angle: never "nothing to wear" again.
2. **Closet** — your wardrobe, digital — show: grid of clothing cutouts by category — layout: screen-grid — angle: your closet in your pocket.
3. **Snap and cut out** — effortless setup — show: phone photo of a shirt turning into a clean cutout, pulled out of the UI — layout: ui-pieces — angle: add a piece in seconds.
4. **AI stylist** — smart — show: weather and occasion inputs becoming an outfit suggestion card — layout: floating-cards — angle: a stylist that knows your closet.
5. **Plan** — routine — show: outfit calendar for the week, or a trip packing list — layout: hero-device — angle: plan your week of outfits.
6. **Try-on or looks** — inspiration — show: three looks on fanned devices (try-on results, or lookbook outfits) — layout: trio-fan — angle: see it before you wear it.
7. **Insights** — smarter wardrobe — show: cost per wear, most worn, unworn for {days} days — layout: floating-cards — angle: shop less, wear more.

## Supporting elements

- Outfit card: top, bottom, shoes and bag cutouts in a flat-lay, with "Today · {temp}°".
- Weather chip: "{temp}° · Light rain". Occasion chips: Work, Date night, Weekend, Travel.
- Closet category tabs: Tops, Bottoms, Dresses, Shoes, Accessories, with item counts.
- Cutout mini comparison: raw phone photo → clean cutout.
- Calendar strip with an outfit thumbnail per day.
- Insight cards: "Most worn: {item}", "Not worn in {days} days", "Cost per wear: {costPerWear}" (the user's own data, not app pricing).
- Packing checklist card for "{tripName} · {days} days".

## Visual direction

- Editorial, luxe, cream and mono: a magazine feel with serif headlines, off-white or beige backgrounds and generous whitespace.
- Clothing cutouts floating with soft shadows. Products are the stars, and device chrome is minimal.
- Inclusive body types, ages and styles across model photos, with no visible designer logos.
- Leaders use Pinterest-style grids, clean cutouts on neutral backgrounds and calendar strips. Low-quality, wrinkled phone photos of clothes undermine the "stylish" promise.

## Avoid

- Visible designer logos, brand monograms or trademarked patterns on demo clothing (Apple 5.2.1, 2.3.9). Influencer or runway photos without rights.
- Body-shaming copy ("hide your flaws", "flatter a problem area"). Use "styles for your shape" instead.
- Virtual try-on results that look better than the real feature delivers, and AI-generated model images without disclosure.
- Affiliate prices, discount codes or "shop now" banners in screenshots.
- Sexualized imagery (Apple 1.1.4, Google Play sexual content policy).
- No invented "{users} stylish users", ratings or "#1 wardrobe app".
- Conversion killers: an empty closet screen as the hook, too much UI chrome, and messy item photos.
