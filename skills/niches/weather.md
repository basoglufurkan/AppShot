# Weather

## The searcher

- Planning tomorrow: commute, school run, outfit, a barbecue — wants to know *when* exactly it will rain, not just "60%".
- Outdoor people with stakes: runners, cyclists, hikers, sailors, fishermen, photographers, farmers.
- Storm and hurricane season worriers who want early, clear alerts and a live radar.
- Allergy and asthma sufferers checking pollen and air quality.
- After installing they want to feel: prepared, no surprises, a little delighted by how good the forecast looks.

## What wins the install

1. **Hyperlocal timing** — "Rain starting in {minutes} min" is the killer feature people switch apps for.
2. **A radar worth watching** — smooth, beautiful precipitation and storm maps.
3. **Alerts that matter** — severe-weather and personal alerts (rain, UV, wind) without noise.
4. **Glanceable** — widgets, Lock Screen and watch: weather without opening the app.
5. **Beyond temperature** — air quality, pollen, UV, wind for health and hobbies.
6. **Beauty** — living skies and elegant data make it the app people keep on their home screen.

## Story (shot by shot)

1. **Hook** — know before you go — show: today's forecast with a big temperature over a living sky — layout: full-bleed — angle: the sky *is* the background; device or UI floats on it.
2. **Rain timing** — rain alerts to the minute — show: minute-by-minute precipitation chart — layout: zoom-callout — angle: zoom on "Rain starts {time}".
3. **Radar** — see storms coming — show: animated radar frame — layout: device-top — angle: crop the map large, storm cell in accent colour.

## Supporting elements

- **Big temperature numeral**: "{temp}°" with condition and high/low.
- **Rain timeline**: minute bars with "Rain starts {time}, stops {time}".
- **Notifications**: "Thunderstorm warning until {time}", "Rain in {minutes} min at your location", "UV index high today".
- **Metric cards**: AQI {aqi} · Pollen {level} · UV {uv} · Wind {wind}.
- **Sunrise/sunset arc** card.
- **Widget mocks**: small, medium, Lock Screen.
- **City cards**: 3 cities with different conditions to show visual range.

## Visual direction

- Living skies: gradients and imagery that change with conditions (bright sky blue, stormy slate, warm sunset, deep night) — sky, ocean, aurora, glass themes.
- Huge, thin numerals for temperature; glass cards for metrics; generous spacing.
- Radar maps full and dark with vivid precipitation colours; keep map attribution visible.
- Leaders: Apple Weather (gradient realism), CARROT (bold personality), Windy (data-rich), AccuWeather/The Weather Channel (alert-led). Personality or polish — pick one.
- Units and formats match the storefront (°C/km/h for most of Europe; °F/mph for US).
- Avoid dramatic disaster photography; let the UI show severity.

## Avoid

- Implying official warnings or using agency logos (NOAA/NWS, Met Office) unless data is licensed and attribution is shown (Apple 5.2.1); data providers like Apple WeatherKit require attribution.
- "Most accurate weather app" or "#1" without independent proof (Apple 2.3.1).
- Real disaster photos or tragic events as marketing — insensitive, and Apple's objectionable-content rules (1.1) reject apps that capitalise on tragedies.
- Location-permission prompts or onboarding as shot 1.
- Wrong units or date formats for the locale.
- Conversion mistakes: tiny 10-day tables, six metrics in one shot, low-contrast white text on pale skies, identical sunny screens across all shots.
