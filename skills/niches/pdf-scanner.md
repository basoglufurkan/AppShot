# PDF & Document Scanner

## The searcher

- Someone who has to send a signed document *right now* — a lease, an HR form, a bank document — and has no printer or scanner.
- A small business owner or freelancer drowning in receipts and paperwork at tax time.
- A student scanning notes, handouts and book pages to study or share.
- In Turkey: scanning kimlik, diplomas, e-Devlet printouts or contracts for official applications.
- After installing they want to feel: done in a minute, and the result looks professional.

## What wins the install

1. **Professional result from a phone photo** — crooked, shadowy photo → crisp, flat, white PDF is the proof that converts.
2. **Sign and send in a minute** — the urgent job; show the signature going on and the share sheet.
3. **Text you can use** — OCR to copy, search and edit text from any page.
4. **All PDF tools in one** — merge, compress, convert, fill forms: removes the need for three other apps.
5. **Organised and findable** — folders, auto-naming, search, cloud export.
6. **Privacy** — on-device processing and no forced account, where true.

## Visual direction

- Clean, bright and trustworthy: white and soft-gray with a blue/ocean accent; documents themselves provide the contrast.
- The before/after diagonal is the signature visual — make the "before" believably bad (angle, shadow, wood table) and the "after" pristine.
- Documents must be fictional and generic (a rental agreement for "Sample Co."), large enough to read headings.
- Minimal type, one message per shot; tools grids with large, consistent icons.
- Leaders (Adobe Scan, Genius Scan, Scanner Pro, CamScanner) lead with capture quality, then signing and OCR.
- Avoid dark, cluttered desk photos — keep the scene light and uncluttered.

## Avoid

- "Legally binding" or "e-imza" claims for drawn signatures — in Turkey e-imza is a qualified electronic signature under Law 5070 with certified hardware; elsewhere legal effect varies by document. Say "sign PDFs", not "legally binding".
- Real IDs, passports or anything mimicking a government document (emblems, real names, ID numbers) — personal data and impersonation risk; use an obviously generic "ID card" mock.
- Apple 4.3 (spam): scanner apps are saturated; screenshots must show genuine differentiation, not a reskin.
- Brand logos of cloud services or Adobe/CamScanner names in captions (5.2.1, 2.3.7).
- "Free" or "unlimited scans" when exports are paywalled; no prices (2.3.7).
- Conversion mistakes: a file-manager list as the hook, a weak "after" image, tool grids with 20 tiny icons.
