# Widgets, Themes & Customization

## The searcher

- A teen or young adult who just saw an aesthetic home-screen setup on TikTok or Pinterest and wants theirs to look like that today.
- Fresh OS update, new Lock Screen/StandBy features — "how do I make it look good?"
- Someone who wants a partner's, best friend's or pet's photo on the Home Screen (social photo widgets).
- Android users after launchers, icon packs, keyboards and fancy fonts for bios and chats.
- Seasonal moods (autumn, Halloween, New Year). After installing they want to feel: "My phone finally looks like *me*" — and get compliments.

## What wins the install

1. **The result itself** — the screenshot *is* the product; a stunning, coherent home screen sells instantly.
2. **Before → after** — the jump from default grid to dreamy setup is the conversion moment.
3. **Variety of vibes** — minimal, cosy, Y2K, dark academia, cute: "there's one for me".
4. **Easy setup** — one-tap theme packs, guided steps; kills "this looks complicated".
5. **Personal and social** — photo widgets, friend widgets, countdowns to dates that matter.
6. **New OS surfaces** — Lock Screen, StandBy, Dynamic Island, keyboards and fonts.

## Visual direction

- The home screen is the hero — show the device large (full-bleed or tilted) and let the setup's own palette lead; match the theme registry to the pack (pastel for cute, aurora for dreamy, neon for Y2K, mono for minimal, glass for clean).
- Every shot must be internally coherent: wallpaper, icons, widgets and fonts in one aesthetic.
- Trendy but readable captions; one short line per shot so the setup stays visible.
- Leaders (Widgetsmith, Colorful Widget, Themify, Brass, Locket-style friend widgets) sell finished looks, not editors.
- Use original or licensed wallpapers and icon art; vary vibes across shots to widen appeal.
- Platform-true visuals: iOS sets show iOS surfaces (StandBy, Dynamic Island); Android sets show launchers and Material You.

## Avoid

- Copyrighted characters, anime, celebrities and brand logos in wallpapers or icon packs (Disney, Sanrio, sports clubs, social-app logos) — the most common rejection here (Apple 5.2.1, Google Play IP policy).
- Promising what the OS doesn't allow: on iOS no true launchers, system-font changes or live wallpapers beyond the OS; "icon changer" works via Shortcuts — be accurate (Apple 2.3.1).
- Apple 4.2/4.3: thin wallpaper-only or clone widget apps get rejected — show real functionality and originality.
- Keyboard extensions: never show sensitive fields; state "Full Access" honestly and respect Apple 4.4.1 keyboard rules.
- Android devices in iOS screenshots or vice versa (2.3.10); prices or "free" when packs are premium (2.3.7).
- Conversion mistakes: tiny devices in big frames, incoherent mixes of styles in one shot, generic stock wallpapers, the editor UI as the hook.
