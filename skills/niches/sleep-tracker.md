# Sleep Tracker

## The searcher

- Someone who wakes up tired despite "enough" hours and wants to understand why. They search "sleep tracker" or "sleep cycle".
- A snorer, or their partner, who is curious (or annoyed) and wants to hear what happens at night.
- Someone who hates jarring alarms and wants to wake gently at the lightest moment.
- Someone who struggles to fall asleep and looks for sounds, stories or a wind-down routine.
- What they want to feel: "I wake up refreshed and finally understand my nights."

## What wins the install

1. **Wake up refreshed.** The outcome, not the tracking. A smart alarm promise is the most persuasive hook in this niche.
2. **The morning report.** A sleep score and stage graph make people curious to see their own.
3. **Night recordings.** Snoring and sleep-talk clips are unique, a little funny, and highly shareable. They're often the feature people tell friends about.
4. **Help falling asleep.** Sounds and stories widen the audience from "trackers" to "can't sleep" searchers.
5. **Personal insights.** Correlations from the user's own nights ("later caffeine, lower score") make the data useful.
6. **Privacy for microphone data.** Saying where recordings live calms a real worry.

## Story (shot by shot)

1. **Hook** — the outcome — show: morning summary with a big sleep score and time asleep — layout: big-number — angle: wake up refreshed.
2. **Smart alarm** — the mechanism — show: alarm screen with a wake-up window — layout: hero-device — angle: rise at your lightest moment.
3. **Stages** — curiosity — show: the hypnogram enlarged, with Awake / REM / Light / Deep bands — layout: zoom-callout — angle: your night, explained.
4. **Recordings** — unique hook — show: snore and sleep-talk audio clip cards with timestamps — layout: floating-cards — angle: hear what happens at night.
5. **Fall asleep** — second audience — show: sound mixer or sleep story player — layout: hero-device — angle: drift off with soothing sounds.
6. **Routine** — habit — show: bedtime reminder and wind-down notifications — layout: notification-stack — angle: build a bedtime you keep.
7. **Insights and trust** — close — show: weekly trends, Health sync, recordings stored on device (only if true) — layout: feature-list — angle: insights from your own nights.

## Supporting elements

- Sleep score ring "{score}", with "Time asleep {hours}h {minutes}m" and bedtime/wake time.
- Hypnogram chart with labeled bands (Awake, REM, Light, Deep) and soft gradient fills.
- Smart alarm window card: "{windowStart} – {windowEnd}".
- Audio clip cards with waveform: "Snoring · {time}", "Sleep talk · {time}", each with a play button.
- Insight card: "You slept {minutes} min longer on workout days", from real tracked data only.
- Sound mixer sliders: Rain, Ocean, Fan, Forest.
- Notification: "Wind down: bedtime in 30 minutes".

## Visual direction

- Deep night palettes: midnight navy, calm indigo, aurora glows, ocean teal. Moon and stars kept subtle, never cartoonish unless the brand is playful.
- Soft glowing charts and rounded sans, with bright accent numerals on low-contrast dark backgrounds. Use a warm sunrise gradient only for the alarm shot.
- Show the device in dark mode throughout. A bright white screenshot in this set feels like a flashlight at 2 a.m.
- Leaders use navy-to-purple gradients, hypnograms, a peaceful sleeper in soft light, and morning-light alarm scenes.

## Avoid

- Diagnostic or treatment claims: "detects sleep apnea", "treats insomnia", "diagnose sleep disorders". That's medical-device territory (FDA, EU MDR) under Apple 1.4.1 and Google Play's health policy. Say "track snoring" and "understand your sleep".
- Accuracy claims for phone-based staging without validation. Apple 1.4.1 asks apps to disclose methodology, and users quickly notice phone-on-mattress limits.
- Recording promises ("never leaves your phone") that don't match the privacy labels and Play Data safety form.
- Sleeping pill, melatonin or supplement promotion.
- Smartwatch brands in screenshots outside their marketing guidelines.
- Prices, trials, invented "{users} sleepers" figures or "#1 sleep app".
- Conversion killers: bright daytime styling, complex charts with no plain-language label, and a feature soup with no outcome in shot 1.
