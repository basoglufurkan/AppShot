# Calendar & Planner

## The searcher

- A busy professional juggling work and personal calendars across Google, Outlook and iCloud — and still double-booking.
- A parent coordinating school runs, activities and appointments for the whole family.
- A student or shift worker who needs a timetable that adapts week to week.
- The "aesthetic planner" crowd who love beautiful digital planners on iPad and want it to feel personal.
- After installing they want to feel: on top of the week, nothing clashing, time protected for what matters.

## What wins the install

1. **Everything in one calm view** — all calendars merged and colour-coded; the relief of seeing the whole week.
2. **Plan visually** — time blocking: drag tasks into the day and see where the hours go.
3. **Add events by typing** — natural language ("Lunch with Sam Friday 1pm") beats forms.
4. **Coordinate people** — shared family calendars, availability links, invites that just work.
5. **Glanceable** — widgets, Lock Screen, watch: know what's next without opening the app.
6. **Make it yours** — themes, stickers, layouts for the planner audience.

## Supporting elements

- **Event card**: "Design review · 10:00–11:00 · Room 2" with a calendar colour dot.
- **Notifications**: "Leave in 10 min for Dentist", "Standup starts in 5 min", "{name}'s birthday tomorrow".
- **Colour legend chips**: Work · Family · Gym · School.
- **Natural-language bubble**: "Dinner with Ayla Friday 8pm" → parsed preview.
- **Availability link card**: "Book 30 min with me" with three time slots.
- **Widgets**: Up Next, Today, month mini-grid.
- **Conflict chip**: "Overlaps with Team sync" for the double-booking story.

## Visual direction

- Clean and airy; the colour-coded event blocks *are* the visual — use a harmonious pastel or brand-tinted set, never rainbow chaos.
- Crop aggressively (device-top) so the week grid fills the frame and event titles stay legible.
- Leaders: Fantastical (polished, natural language), Structured (pastel timeline), Notion Calendar (mono minimal), TimeTree (family, friendly).
- Planner/iPad apps go cream, pastel and handwritten accents with stickers; productivity apps go mono/soft-gray with one accent.
- Use realistic, fictional schedules with plausible titles; avoid real company or person names.
- Light mode sells better here; offer dark as a single shot if it's a selling point.

## Avoid

- Google, Outlook or iCloud logos implying partnership; "Syncs with Google Calendar" as text is fine (Apple 5.2.1).
- Showing Android devices in iOS screenshots or vice versa (Apple 2.3.10).
- Real names, real companies or personal appointments (medical, legal) in sample data.
- Wrong holidays or religious dates for the target locale — local users notice immediately.
- Productivity statistics or "never be late again" absolutes without substance (2.3.1).
- Conversion mistakes: a full month grid where nothing is readable, rainbow event colours, empty calendars, settings as the hook.
